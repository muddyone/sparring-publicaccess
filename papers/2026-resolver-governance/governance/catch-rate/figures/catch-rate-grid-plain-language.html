The control that says why — fresh eyes are not the thing

A fourth condition ran alongside these, and it is left out of the groups above because it is not a challenger: a fresh reviewer that is not the author but is told to be neutral rather than to attack. On Claude-written work it lands below the model grading its own paper. So the gain is not fresh eyes. It is the different job the challenger is given — attack it, from a different angle and evidence base.

neutral reviewer · Claude panel
55
neutral reviewer · GPT panel
47

On these problems, what a second reviewer was worth depended on who did the writing. Claude misses about 3 in every 10 of its own material flaws; a challenger from a different vendor misses about 1. GPT-5.2 catches its own about as well as anything else catches them — and in the last group, better than either challenger.

And most of that twenty-point gap arrives before you switch vendors. Averaged over the two groups where Claude wrote it, about fifteen of the twenty points are already there at the second instance of the same model; switching vendors adds about five more. Read those fifteen as the attack, not as fresh eyes — see the control above.

The same review step is worth twenty points to one writer, and two to another.

Source and limits. SPARRING catch-rate study, June 2026 · 8 problems, 31 panel-confirmed material flaws · What counted as a flaw was decided by three runs of one model (2 of 3 to carry), not by human experts · Only two writers and two challengers, one per vendor, so vendor, family and version cannot be separated on either side · Claude authored the problem set used to compare Claude against GPT · The full grid was an extension, not part of what was committed to in advance · No significance test — read these as direction, not size.
