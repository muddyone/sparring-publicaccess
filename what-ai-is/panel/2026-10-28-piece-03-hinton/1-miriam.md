# Miriam Vale: argument-and-evidence review, essay 03 Hinton passage (2026-10-28)

_Scope: essay 03 ("Something is there. Very probably not conscious."), from "Others put it far
higher." to "So on this question, I weigh Chalmers more.", plus the edited "How this essay was
made" note and the Sources list. Read independently of the other reviewers. Evidence used: brief 16
(Hinton's view), the 2026-10-28 cite-check report, and my own fetch of the full text of Dreksler et
al. 2025 (arXiv 2506.11945, HTML version) and the Globe and Mail 2024 interview page, both on
2026-10-28._

## Overall diagnosis

The passage does what Bart asked, and the register is right. Hinton gets his credentials in full,
his reason in his own words, and a respectful line on where his word carries weight. The quotes
trace. The Globe and Mail paraphrase is faithful in context: what Hinton calls "rubbish" is the
picture of experience as an inner theatre that sets us apart from machines, and the essay says
exactly that.

The weak scaffolding is the second paragraph. It makes two moves that pull against each other. It
uses a survey of AI researchers to show that Chalmers's numbers are in line with the field, then
argues that AI researchers are not the people to ask about consciousness. And it states the survey
more broadly than the paper does. Both are fixable without changing the message, and the fixes
make the conclusion stronger.

**Strongest element.** "On how these systems learn, Hinton's word carries great weight." It concedes
what is true, which earns the turn that follows.

**Biggest credibility risk.** The survey sentence (findings 1 to 3). A reader who opens Dreksler
finds that only 265 of the 582 were asked the dated question, that the figures for today are
Chalmers's mainstream-assumption numbers and not his own, and that the respondents rated technical
AI researchers' expertise *above* philosophers' on this very question.

## Findings, ranked by severity

### 1. The survey's sample is overstated. Impact 7.

**Text:** "In 2024, 582 researchers who publish at leading AI venues were asked how likely it was
that an AI system with subjective experience would exist by 2024, 2034 and 2100. Their median
answers were 1%, 25% and 70%"

**Issue.** The survey asked the timing question in two framings, assigned at random. Only the
"fixed date" group was asked for likelihoods in 2024, 2034 and 2100, and that group was 265 AI
researchers (Dreksler et al. 2025, Table 5: n = 265 for each year). The other group was asked for the
year by which they gave 10%, 50% and 90%. So "582 … were asked … Their median answers were" is not
what was run. The abstract's compression ("When asked to estimate the chances…") is where the
cite-check got 582 from, which is why the checker passed it. It is a small error, but it is a number,
and the essay series lives on its numbers being right. Also, the 2024 question was framed as
"today" (whether AI systems currently have it), which is the comparison the essay actually needs.

**Proposed:** "In May 2024, a survey reached 582 researchers who publish at leading AI venues. Those
asked how likely it was that an AI system with subjective experience exists today, in 2034 and in
2100 gave median answers of 1%, 25% and 70%"

(If the sub-sample size is wanted: "The 265 of them asked how likely…".)

### 2. The survey is made to vouch for Chalmers, which the argument then undercuts. Impact 7.

**Text:** "Chalmers's figures sit near those; Hinton's "already, as much as we do" does not. On how
these systems learn, Hinton's word carries great weight. But whether anyone is experiencing
anything is a question about consciousness, and that is Chalmers's field, not Hinton's."

**Issue.** The paragraph's conclusion is that expertise in how AI learns doesn't make someone an
authority on whether AI experiences anything. The survey is a survey of exactly that expertise. So
offering it as support for Chalmers ("Chalmers's figures sit near those") borrows authority from
the group the next sentence says is not the authority. A sharp reader notices. Two further facts
from the paper make it worse if left as is:

- The authors say their forecasts are "more useful for understanding current attitudes rather than
  for predicting technological development", and that respondents' ideas of "subjective
  experience" may be folk ideas that differ from those of "experts who study the science and
  philosophy of mind" (Dreksler et al. 2025, limitations).
- The respondents rated technical AI researchers among the *most* important sources of expertise
  on whether an AI has subjective experience, with philosophers of mind "to a slightly lesser
  degree". A reader who finds that will feel the essay cited a survey whose respondents would
  disagree with its conclusion.

The survey does do one job cleanly, and that job fits Bart's intent: it shows Hinton is not
speaking for his field. A clip labelled "the godfather of AI" invites the reader to hear the field's
verdict, and the survey shows the field's typical answer is far lower. Give it that job and only
that job. Chalmers's standing then rests on the domain argument, which is where it belongs.

The paper's own limitations note gives the cleanest bridge into the domain point, and it is
generous to everyone: AI researchers are not claiming the last word either.

**Proposed** (replacing from "Chalmers's figures sit near those" to the end of the paragraph):

"So when Hinton says AI is already conscious, he is not reporting the view of his field; its
typical answer is far lower. Nor does the survey settle the question; its authors say it shows what
researchers think, not what is true. On how these systems learn, Hinton's word carries great
weight. Whether anyone is experiencing anything is a question about consciousness, which is
Chalmers's field more than Hinton's. So on this question, I weigh Chalmers more."

(See finding 4 for the last two sentences. If Bart wants to keep the comparison to Chalmers's
numbers, see finding 3 for an accurate form, placed after the field point so it reads as
consistency, not corroboration.)

### 3. "Chalmers's figures" are not his own figures, and "near" is loose. Impact 6.

**Text:** "Chalmers's figures sit near those"

**Issue.** Two paragraphs earlier the essay takes care to say that "under 10 percent" and "25
percent or more" are what mainstream assumptions imply, "not his personal view; his own estimates
were somewhat higher on both counts". "Chalmers's figures" undoes that caveat a few lines later.
And on "today", "under 10 percent" against a median of 1% is a bound that contains the median, not a
figure near it. The ten-year numbers (25% or more by about 2033; median 25% by 2034) are genuinely
close.

**Proposed, if the comparison is kept:** "The figures Chalmers reached on mainstream assumptions are
consistent with those; Hinton's "already, as much as we do" is not."

### 4. The domain move is sound as weighting, but "not Hinton's" overstates it, and it argues for a field, not one man. Impact 6.

**Text:** "that is Chalmers's field, not Hinton's. So on this question, I weigh Chalmers more."

**Issue, and why the move still stands.** Giving more weight to someone working in the relevant
field is a legitimate way to weigh testimony, and "weigh more" (not "Chalmers is right") is the
correctly narrow conclusion. It also fits the series' yardstick essay, which says expert judgment
is "valuable, and it is not the gold standard we imagine": the passage weighs, it doesn't defer,
and it engages Hinton's reason before it weighs him. Good.

Two things overreach:

- **"Not Hinton's" is too absolute.** The question of AI consciousness is multidisciplinary by any
  account, including the cited survey's respondents. Hinton's first degree was in experimental
  psychology and his career is in cognitive science as much as computing (the brief itself says
  "with roots in cognitive psychology"). His account of experience is not an amateur's. It sits
  near positions held inside philosophy of mind (deflationary and illusionist views). Calling the
  question wholly outside his field invites the rejoinder and weakens a point that holds in its
  narrower form: consciousness is Chalmers's central subject, and learning is Hinton's. "More than
  Hinton's" says that and cannot be rebutted.
- **The reason supports weighting the field, and the field here is not only Chalmers.** Seth,
  Tononi and Koch are also in it, and the essay has just said they put the chance lower than
  Chalmers does. That actually helps Bart: everyone the essay cites from the study of
  consciousness, whether nearer Chalmers or Seth, puts today's chance low. Saying so makes "far
  higher" for Hinton land harder and makes "I weigh Chalmers more" read as the essay's existing
  position, not a pick of one authority. Optional, but it is the stronger argument.

**Proposed (minimal):** "Whether anyone is experiencing anything is a question about consciousness,
which is Chalmers's field more than Hinton's. So on this question, I weigh Chalmers more."

**Proposed (stronger, optional):** "Whether anyone is experiencing anything is a question about
consciousness, Chalmers's field more than Hinton's, and those who study it, from Chalmers to Seth,
put today's chance low. So on this question, I weigh Chalmers more."

### 5. "An argument, not a finding" applies to Chalmers too; say so, and say what follows. Impact 5.

**Text:** "That view of experience is disputed, and it is an argument, not a finding."

**Issue.** True, and the brief marks it as ours. But the essay has already said that Chalmers's
neuron-replacement step is "the contested step" and that its own estimate is "a judgment, his and
mine, not a measurement". Said of Hinton alone, "an argument, not a finding" reads as a demotion
that is not applied to the other side. The register rule (generous, facts not softened) is better
served by saying it of both, which costs nothing factually. There is also a fair and clarifying
point worth one clause: Hinton's conclusion does follow from his account, since if experience is
only a way of talking about what perception reports, a system with perception has it. The dispute
is the premise. Saying that is generous to Hinton and sharpens why it is a question about
consciousness, not about learning, which sets up finding 4.

**Proposed:** "If he is right about what experience is, his conclusion follows. But that view of
experience is disputed, and like Chalmers's, it is an argument, not a finding."

### 6. "Most of his own field" leans on one survey. Impact 4.

**Text:** "It also sits far from most of his own field."

**Issue.** Defensible as far as it goes: in the dated group the median for "today" was 1% and three
quarters answered 10% or less (Table 5, Q3 = 10), so Hinton's near-certainty is far from most
respondents. But it is one survey, of a sample of conference authors with a 6.2% response rate,
asked about "an AI system", which is a weaker claim than Hinton's "just as much as we do". "Most
of his own field" states a fact about the field. The survey supports a fact about the survey.

**Proposed:** "It also sits far from what most researchers in his field said in the largest survey
on the question I know of."

(If "largest" can't be sourced, drop it: "…in a large survey of his field.")

### 7. A primary-text quote exists and is not used. Impact 3.

**Text:** ""AI has just as much subjective experience as we do," he said in October 2026
([Futurism 2026] … reporting a Telegraph interview)."

**Issue.** Not an error; the essay flags the secondary sourcing honestly. But brief 16 has a
quote checked against its own page: LBC, 28 January 2026, "Multimodal AI already has subjective
experiences…". Using a quote you have verified at the source is better than one quoted via a
reporter, and the "how this essay was made" caveat could then go. The catch: the LBC quote goes on
"…if we weren't talking to philosophers, we'd agree that AI was aware", which would need care not
to read as a jab in either direction (though it does show Hinton himself sees the question as one
philosophers dispute). Bart's call. If adopted, it needs a cite-check run and a Sources entry.

**Proposed (option):** ""Multimodal AI already has subjective experiences," he said in January
2026 ([LBC 2026](https://www.lbc.co.uk/tech/ai-consciousness-geoffrey-hinton/)), and in October
that "AI has just as much subjective experience as we do" ([Futurism 2026] …, reporting a
Telegraph interview)."

### 8. "That is probably Geoffrey Hinton" holds. Impact 2 (no change).

The "godfathers of AI" label is shared with Bengio and LeCun (The Verge 2019), so "probably" is the
right hedge, and neither of the other two holds the "already conscious" view in public. The clip
that prompted the passage was Hinton's (brief 16). Keep.

### 9. Notes and Sources. Impact 2.

- **How this essay was made:** accurate as written. It might add that the survey figures were
  checked against the paper's full text, not only the abstract, once finding 1 is applied; the
  cite-check report's evidence for c07 was the abstract, which is how the 582 slipped through.
  Suggested sentence after "…could not be fetched.": "The survey's figures were checked against the
  paper's full text."
- **Sources list ordering:** "The Guardian 2023" is filed under G, "The Verge 2019" under T (after
  Tononi). Pick one rule; filing both by the publication name without "The" puts The Verge after
  Tononi & Koch anyway, so only The Guardian's position needs a check against the house rule. Trivial.

## Does it contradict anything elsewhere in the essay?

- **The mainstream-assumptions caveat:** yes, as written ("Chalmers's figures"). Finding 3.
- **"A judgment, his and mine, not a measurement":** a near-contradiction in tone, not in fact.
  Finding 5.
- **"What would change my mind" ("New reasons, from Chalmers or anyone, that move the estimate"):**
  consistent, because the passage engages Hinton's reason before weighing his authority. The
  findings above keep it that way; dropping the reason and keeping only the credential would break it.
- **The definition of "conscious" ("someone is experiencing something: that there is something it
  is like to be it"):** Hinton's account denies the inner theatre that this definition points at, so
  he and the essay partly mean different things by "subjective experience". The passage's "His case
  rests on a view of what experience is" already covers this; finding 5's clause makes it explicit.

## Verdict

The passage earns its conclusion on the argument, not on the credential, which is the version of
this move worth making. Fix the survey's sample size and Chalmers's figures (findings 1 and 3),
give the survey the single job of showing Hinton doesn't speak for his field (finding 2), and
narrow "not Hinton's" to "more than Hinton's" (finding 4). Then "I weigh Chalmers more" lands as
inevitable rather than asserted. None of this needs a new part; the paragraph stays two paragraphs.
