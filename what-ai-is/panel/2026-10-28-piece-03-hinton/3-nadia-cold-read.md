# Nadia Okonkwo, cold read: essay 03, the Hinton passage

_Read once, straight through, essay only. 2026-10-28. Focus: "Others put it far higher." to "So on
this question, I weigh Chalmers more."_

---

The opening works. "Others put it far higher" right after "lower still" is a clean turn, and "if
you have seen a clip of the godfather of AI" is the first moment in the essay that sounds like it
knows I exist. I have seen that clip. I sat up.

Then I hit the credentials paragraph and slowed down. Turing Award: I don't really know what it is,
but sitting next to a Nobel I assume it's a big prize, so I moved on. It didn't stop me. What did
slow me was that it's one long sentence with three links in it, and it reads like a Wikipedia box
dropped in. By the end of it I'd half forgotten what he said.

**Where I actually stopped and reread:** the Hinton quote. "Subjective experience is just a way of
talking about what your perceptual system's telling you when it's not working 'properly'." I read
it twice. First time I took it to mean experience only happens when something's broken, which
seemed like a strange thing to say, and I couldn't see how that gets you to "AI has just as much
subjective experience as we do." The essay doesn't connect the two. So I know he has a theory, I
know he thinks inner experience is "rubbish", but I still don't know *why that makes the chatbot
conscious*. That's the bit I wanted, and it's the bit that's missing.

"That view of experience is disputed, and it is an argument, not a finding." This felt like a door
closing. Disputed by who? It's one line, and it shuts him down before I've understood him.

**The survey.** "Researchers who publish at leading AI venues": I read "venues" as places, like
conference halls, then worked out it means journals or conferences. It sounds like insider talk.
"By 2024, 2034 and 2100", asked *in* 2024: I had to stop on "by 2024". Oh, it means "already".
Just say "already". Median: I know it roughly means the typical answer; fine. Then I had to match
1%, 25%, 70% back to the three dates in my head. I got there: about a 1 in 100 chance it exists
now, 1 in 4 within ten years, better than even by 2100. But I did the work, the sentence didn't.
And "Chalmers's figures sit near those": his figures were several paragraphs up. I scrolled back.
"Under 10 percent" versus 1% being "near" was a bit generous, but okay.

**Why Chalmers over Hinton.** I got it: Hinton knows how these things learn, Chalmers knows
consciousness. It's not a put-down. The credentials paragraph is respectful, almost too respectful.
But two things nagged me:

- The essay uses Hinton's own field against him ("far from most of his own field"), then in the
  next breath says consciousness isn't his field. If it's not an AI researchers' question, why does
  a survey of AI researchers settle anything? I noticed that on first read. A sharp TikTok fan would
  too.
- The essay opened with "my position is largely Chalmers's". So "I weigh Chalmers more" lands as
  "I agree with the person I already agree with." It's honest, but it doesn't feel like a reason.
  The reason is the field point, and that's one short sentence.

**Bolted on or part of it?** Mostly part of it. It slots into the "how likely is it" section
naturally. The seam is after it: "Chalmers draws a practical lesson anyway." "Anyway" made me
think, anyway what, despite Hinton? It reads as if the Hinton bit was inserted and the old next
paragraph wasn't told.

**Would the TikTok viewer feel answered?** Half. They'd learn who he is, that he's serious, and
that most researchers don't agree with him. That's useful. But they'd come away feeling *outranked*
rather than *answered*: "a philosopher and a poll disagree with your guy." Nobody shows them, in
plain words, what Hinton's argument is and where it goes wrong. If I'd come in believing him, I'd
leave still believing him, just knowing I'm in the minority.

Would I share it? The rest of the essay, maybe. This passage, I'd skim the middle.
