**Rosetta: "What I tested" (essay 02)**

**Verdict:** The section is clear and in the right order: question, steps, result, meaning, limits. Both callbacks land. There are two problems a listener would notice. The essay mentions a "third version" it never counted out, and the score "2 without them" has no unit. The rest is small fixes for the ear.

1. **"the result came only on the third version of the task"** → "the result came only after I had made the task harder twice"
   The section names two versions ("the first test", "a harder version"), so a reader can't find a third. Your full account may well have three. If so, the fix is to name the middle one in paragraph two, not to change the count. **Impact 8**

2. **"10 times out of 10, against 2 without them"** → "10 times out of 10, against 2 out of 10 without them"
   Heard aloud, a bare "2" has no unit. **Impact 7**

3. **"(light up only when two particular blocks are on together)"** → ". That is, they light up only when two particular blocks are on together. They expect"
   A voice reads a parenthesis as flat words in the middle of the sentence. It's also a repeat, since the blicket bullet already defined "needs two." You could cut it instead. **Impact 6**

4. **"where the rules were never named"** → "where nobody told the models what kinds of rule to look for"
   "Named" is unclear: named to whom, and by whom? Keep my words only if they match what the study actually did. **Impact 6**

5. **"with the rule held in writing rather than in the learner?"** → "keeping the rule in its notes rather than in its head?"
   "In the learner" is abstract, and "its head" is plain speech. Note this only works if "the learner" here means the model, not the assistant as a whole. If "head" sits badly with the essay's "assistant as a whole" point, say "rather than inside the model." **Impact 5**

6. **"three current models already got the rule right on every machine, so notes had nothing to add"** → "the three models I tried already got the rule right on every machine, so notes had nothing to add"
   "Current models" sounds like a term of art, and it leaves the listener asking "which ones?" **Impact 4**

7. **"which is the gap Gopnik's group found, closed for one model on one task"** → "That was the gap Gopnik's group found, and here it closed, for one model on one task."
   The tacked-on "closed for…" is hard to follow by ear. Two clauses make the result clear. **Impact 5**

8. **"Its notes held the rule, like the rats' map, until the next machine called for it."** → "Its notes held the rule, the way the rats held their map of the maze, until the next machine called for it."
   This callback comes about 160 lines after Tolman. A listener needs the maze back to make the link. **Impact 4**

**Whole-section pass**
- **Kinds of number:** one, an x-out-of-10 count. "Four tries" is a setting, not a result. The load is fine.
- **Sends the reader elsewhere:** only "the full account" link, which is optional. Nothing here depends on it.
- **Honest claims:** good. The limits paragraph says it's one study, raises the same-company question, and ends on "suggests, short of proving."
