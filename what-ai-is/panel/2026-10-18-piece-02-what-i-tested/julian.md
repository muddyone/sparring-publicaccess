**Julian Crest: review of "What I tested"**

**Verdict.** This reads as an argument, not as a study summary. It opens with a question that "Her claim, tested" leaves open: none of those studies tests learning across tasks. The second paragraph gives the result. The third says what the result means: the notes made exploring efficient, the rule was discovered rather than received, and the notes held it like the rats' map. Both callbacks land as payoffs, not decoration, and the hand-off from "That second half is her claim, still standing" to "None of these studies…" is clean.

There are three problems. Two references were left over from the long account. The limits paragraph has lost the word the next section depends on. And the "needs two" rule gets defined a second time.

**Leftovers elsewhere.** I searched the whole essay. "Four tests" appears only in the Notes, where it describes the citation checker ("It ran four times"), so it is fine. Line 77 ("That is what my study tested (below)") and line 173 still point correctly. The only orphans are inside this section, and edits 2, 3, 4 and 5 fix them.

**Line edits**

1. "Children who see several machines that all "need two" (light up only when two particular blocks are on together) expect the same of the next one." → "Children who see several machines that all "need two" expect the same of the next one."
   *Reason:* the blicket list defined "needs two" ten lines earlier, so the reader stumbles on the repeat.

2. "right pair of blocks on the final machine 10 times out of 10" → "right pair of blocks on the last machine in the series 10 times out of 10"
   *Reason:* the section never sets up a series of machines, so "the final machine" points at nothing.

3. "against 2 without them" → "against 2 in 10 without them"
   *Reason:* a bare "2" makes the reader stop and work out "2 what".

4. "the result came only on the third version of the task" → "the result came only after I had reshaped the task twice"
   *Reason:* the section now describes two versions ("the first test", "a harder version"), so "third" refers to a count the reader never saw. The fact stays the same.

5. "It is one small study," → "It is one small, exploratory study, not planned in advance,"
   *Reason:* "What would change my mind" opens with "a repeat of the study, planned in advance". That only works if this paragraph has said the first run wasn't planned in advance. The long account said so, and the cut removed it. "Where I land" also calls the study "exploratory".

6. "nobody tested whether the rule gives way when the pattern breaks" → "nobody tested whether the rule gives way when the pattern changes"
   *Reason:* it matches the wording in the next section ("doesn't give way when the pattern changes"), so the reader sees it is the same limit being picked up.

**One flag, not an edit.** The claim that children expect a repeated rule to hold for the next machine has no source attached in this section. If it comes from Lucas et al. 2014, a short citation keeps it inside the accuracy gate. If it comes from somewhere else, the citation checker should see it before this ships.
