**The reader (required field):** an everyday AI user. Someone who uses ChatGPT or Claude at work or at home, has
heard that "AI is making us stupid", and wonders whether leaning on it is costing them their own skills. Many
manage people or have been managed; some are early in their careers or have children who are. They are not
researchers. They will **act on** the six-step list in "What I'd do with this: what good managers do", and on
one rule (choose on purpose which skills to keep, starting with the ones you need to judge the work, and keep
learning them on purpose). Every number is a reason to believe the argument, so it must be understood on first
read, labelled with what it counts, on a scale the reader knows.

## 3. The register target

State the finding, then say what it means in plainer words. Checks: about 30% of sentences under eight words; at
least three explainer connectives; read every paragraph aloud. **Flow outranks the short-sentence ratio:**
connected prose, each paragraph opening from where the last ended, fragments only as a deliberate landing. Flag
a register failure the way you would flag an overclaim, in both directions: nothing demeaning toward anyone's
work (including the researchers and companies named), nothing self-deprecating about ours. Authorial presence is
something to protect: it is Bart's essay, in his voice. American spelling; serial comma always.
