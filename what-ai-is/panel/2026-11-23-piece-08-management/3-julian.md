## Diagnosis

The reframe is right, and it is half done. The management picture gives the essay something the earlier draft
didn't have: a situation the reader has lived, which turns "you'll lose skills" from a threat into an ordinary
career move with known habits attached. Where the frame is used to *think*, it produces the essay's best
sentences. The clearest case is "In management terms: what happens to people who are asked to direct the work
before they've ever done it?" The earlier draft could not have asked that question.

But the essay's skeleton is still the earlier draft's: learned, kept, check, feel, juniors, limits, advice. The
frame was laid over it, mostly at the seams. Count where "manage" occurs: about 30 times, clustered in the
opening, the first two paragraphs of the first trap, the comparison section, and the close. Through the long
middle (the history, both "What fades" sections, the second trap) it appears only as a **tag sentence at the
end of a paragraph or section**: "That's the same pattern as the move into management", "This is the part every
new manager knows", "But anyone who has made that move will recognize it", "for a manager and for anyone using
AI". In one place the tag replaced a better line the earlier draft had (finding 2). The evidence in those
sections is about students, doctors, pilots, and developers, which is fine. What's missing is any moment where
the reader *sees* a manager. The frame is asserted every few hundred words and never once shown.

So flag 2's answer is: **the frame carries the position, the first trap, and the close; it is bolted on across
the middle.** The fix is not more tags. It is fewer tags, one concrete management picture early (Bart's to
supply), and a section order that lets the management comparison and its limits sit together.

Momentum: strong opening paragraph; a dip in the intro, which opens calculators twice and makes two promises it
pays in different places; good climb through "What fades: where the doing was the learning" (its last line is
the best in the essay); steady through the first trap to its peak at Dell'Acqua and Leyden & Scur; a sag in the
second trap's last two paragraphs; a lift at the juniors question; then the advice, whose best evidence (Bart's
own "done" moment) arrives after the six steps; and a close that restates the position word for word.

Measured: about 4,000 words of body before the Notes; 24% of sentences under eight words (target about 30%). The
prose is connected and reads aloud well, so per the brief the ratio is not a finding.

## Ranked findings

### 1. The frame is asserted through the middle, never shown: the essay needs one management picture (Impact 8; flag 2; additive)

**Passage:** the opening's "The good managers I've known chose which ones to keep sharp, and how to do it.";
"What fades: where the doing kept the skill": "This is the part every new manager knows." and "I found no study
that measures this in people who moved into management. But anyone who has made that move will recognize it."

**Problem.** The title and the position ask the reader to see their AI use as a move into management. The essay
then gives them no manager to look at. "The good managers I've known" is the only first-hand management line,
and it's a summary: no one, no skill, no moment. Every later appeal to the frame leans on the reader's own
recognition ("every new manager knows", "anyone who has made that move will recognize it"). Brief 14b says,
correctly, that no study measures managers' own skills fading, and the essay says so. That honesty is exactly
why the recognition has to be *earned* on the page: with no study, an example is the only support the frame
has. Readers who have never managed (the brief names early-career readers) get nothing to recognize.

**Fix (additive; Bart's to supply, no source needed).** One short, concrete management moment from Bart's own
working life: a skill he or a manager he worked with let fade after moving up, and one they chose to keep,
and why. Put it in the opening, straight after "When you're promoted to run the team…", replacing the summary
sentence, or at the top of "What fades: where the doing kept the skill", replacing "This is the part every new
manager knows." With it in place, "anyone who has made that move will recognize it" becomes a callback, not a
request for the reader's agreement. I'm not drafting the moment, because it has to be his and true.

What it adds for the reader: the frame's whole promise, a familiar situation with known habits, is only kept if
the reader can picture the situation. This is the one passage the essay most needs to grow.

### 2. The tag sentences: cut them, and restore the line one of them replaced (Impact 7; flag 2)

**Passages:**
- "That's the same pattern as the move into management. You don't get worse at everything. You get worse at the
  parts you've stopped doing." (end of the history)
- "AI follows the same pattern. Here's where the cost lands." (two paragraphs later)
- "This is the part every new manager knows."
- "Staying close to the work takes effort, for a manager and for anyone using AI." (end of the second trap)

**Problem.** Each one joins a section to the frame by announcing the join. Two of them use "the same pattern"
eight lines apart, and the two patterns are different: the first is the tools' pattern (you lose the skill the
tool took over), the second follows the specialists paragraph, so it reads as "you rely on others' skills and
don't need your own". The reader can't tell which pattern AI is said to follow, and the second reading
contradicts the essay's advice. The last tag replaced the earlier draft's landing, "A tool built to keep you
sharp won't win on how pleasant it is to use." That line had content (the friction is a design fact, so don't
expect the market to fix it). The tag has only a link.

**Fix.**
- Keep "You don't get worse at everything. You get worse at the parts you've stopped doing." as the history's
  landing. It already sounds like the management experience without having to say so. Cut the sentence
  before it, or fold it in: "That's the move into management too: you don't get worse at everything…"
- Cut "AI follows the same pattern." Open the next section on its own first line ("The strongest evidence is
  about people learning something new.") or bridge with "Here's where the cost lands for AI."
- Replace "This is the part every new manager knows." with the picture from finding 1, or cut it.
- Restore the earlier landing for the second trap, or something with its content. If the frame must appear
  there, it should do work: "Staying close to the work is effort a manager chooses; no tool will choose it for
  you" (sample only; the drafter rewrites).

The frame survives losing all four tags. Because they're its weakest uses, it reads stronger without them.

### 3. The specialists set-up sits five sections from its pay-off, and until then it reads as "you don't need the skills" (Impact 6; flag 3, structural side)

**Passage:** end of "We've had this worry before": "I don't do my own taxes; I use an accountant. … I don't
have those skills, and I don't need them. Leaning on other people's skills is how working life has always run."
Pay-off in "Where the comparison needs care": "The specialists I rely on are different again. … AI has nothing
like that yet that you can count on."

**Problem.** This is a third frame. The essay has tools (calculators, GPS), management, and now
specialists, and the specialists passage arrives last in the history section, where it gets the section's final
word. The reader carries "I don't need them" through both "What fades" sections and both traps, which argue the
opposite for the skills that matter. The correction comes about 2,000 words later. Whether the passage supports
the frame is Miriam's and Nadia's call. Structurally, a set-up this far from its turn reads as a
contradiction, not a set-up.

**Fix.** Move the specialists passage whole into "Where the comparison needs care", as its own short beat:
"I don't keep an accountant's skills… because licenses, track records… do the judging. AI has nothing like
that yet." Contrast and pay-off then land in one place, and the history section ends on its pattern (finding
2). If Bart wants the reassurance early, keep a single clause in the history ("…the way I lean on an accountant
for my taxes") and leave the argument for later.

### 4. "Where the comparison needs care" opens as a retreat, says "two differences", and gives four (Impact 6; flags 2 and 4)

**Passage:** "The management comparison is useful, but it isn't exact, and two differences matter." It is
followed by four paragraphs: the team keeps the skill; a team pushes back (Köbis); "So be the manager, but
don't think of the AI as an employee" (Wiles); the specialists.

**Problem.** Two craft defects, and one opportunity the section misses. The count is wrong: a reader who is
counting finds four. The opening sounds like the frame giving ground just before the advice that rests on
it. The opportunity is that every difference in the section breaks the same way: the team's skill isn't there,
the pushback isn't there, the licenses and records aren't there, so **more of the judging falls on you**. That
doesn't undercut the title. It is the strongest reason for its second half, "Choose the skills you keep". The
section's last paragraph reaches it ("more of the judging stays with you, and so does more of the skill it
takes"), but only at the end.

**Fix.** Open with the direction, and drop the count: "The comparison isn't exact. But every place it breaks,
it breaks the same way: more of the judging stays with you." (sample). Then the differences, then the
specialists (finding 3), with no number to keep track of.

### 5. "Be the manager, but don't think of the AI as an employee" leaves the reader managing nobody (Impact 6; additive)

**Passage:** "So be the manager, but don't think of the AI as an employee."

**Problem.** It's the correct lesson (brief 14b: "tell them they are the manager while saying plainly that the
AI is not an employee"). As written, though, it's a riddle. To most readers a manager is defined by having
employees, so the sentence takes away the half of the picture they'd use to apply it. It needs one sentence
saying what "being the manager" means *here*: the manager's habits, not the manager's relationship. You're
responsible for judging the work, you stay close enough to tell good from bad, and you don't give it the trust
you'd give a colleague who has earned it.

**Fix (adds one sentence).** "What carries over is the manager's job, not the manager's relationship: you
still judge the work and answer for it, but the trust you'd build with a colleague over time isn't there to
lean on" (sample; check against brief 14b §5.4 and §5.5 before adopting). What it adds: the reader can apply the
title without the misreading the Wiles study warns about.

### 6. The advice section is headed "what good managers do", and its evidence is pilots and classrooms (Impact 6; flag 2)

**Passage:** "## What I'd do with this: what good managers do", then "Pilots offer a model." and "The education
research points the same way."

**Problem.** The heading makes a promise about managers. The section keeps it with the FAA, Bastani, Gajos,
Danry, and Shen & Tamkin, none of which is about managers. That's honest evidence for the habits, but the gap
between heading and support is the frame showing its bolt again, right where the reader is told to act.

**Fix (adds a sentence or two).** Bridge it in the open: "No study I found tests how managers keep their skills
sharp. The best evidence for how to do it comes from pilots and classrooms, and it says the same thing good
managers do: keep the tool, and schedule the practice." (sample; it reuses the FAA paragraph's own landing,
"They kept the tool and scheduled the practice.") Alternatively, shorten the heading to "What I'd do with
this", the series' form in 06 and 07, and let step 3 carry the management line.

### 7. The essay's best first-hand moment comes after the six steps (Impact 6)

**Passage:** "And in my own work, I've had AI tools report that a job was done when it hadn't happened. What
caught it was knowing what the work should have produced."

**Problem.** It's the first trap from Bart's own experience, and it's the most manager-shaped moment in the
essay: something reported done, and only the person who knew the work could tell it wasn't. Placed after the
list, it reads as a footnote to step 6. My 07 report found the same pattern there (`panel/2026-11-18-piece-07-
agents/3-julian.md`, finding 1): the scene that *is* the argument, spent late.

**Fix.** Move it to the top of "The first trap: judging work you no longer do", before "A manager's main job…",
told in two or three sentences of what happened (no numbers, per brief §4). Keep the Resolver-paper sentence
where it is, under "I try to hold myself to the same rule", since that one is about the remedy. If finding 1's
picture goes in the opening, this becomes the second concrete moment, which the essay can carry at its length.

### 8. Section order: let the comparison and its limits sit next to the advice (Impact 5; flag 1)

**Current order:** worry before → fades (learning) → fades (kept) → first trap → second trap → comparison needs
care → juniors → doesn't show → advice.

**Problem.** "Where the comparison needs care" ends on the essay's best hand-off to the advice ("more of the
judging stays with you, and so does more of the skill it takes"). Then the juniors section and the limits
section stand between it and the steps. Juniors belongs with the traps anyway: its question ("How do they
become the experts who can check the AI?") follows from the first trap and is asked in management terms.

**Fix.** Worry before → fades (learning) → fades (kept) → first trap → second trap → **juniors** → **comparison
needs care** → doesn't show → advice. Combined with finding 9, the limits section shrinks to the short-term
paragraph, and the care section's last line can lead nearly straight into "The question isn't whether to hand
work to AI."

### 9. The opening promises "the loudest version rests on the weakest evidence" and pays it in section 8 (Impact 5)

**Passage:** opening: "But 'it makes you stupid' is too broad to be useful, and the loudest version of it rests
on the weakest evidence." Paid in "What the evidence doesn't show" (Kosmyna, "brain rot").

**Problem.** The intro sets up "AI is making us stupid" as the worry, and the history section answers it with the
old alarms. The *modern* alarm, the one the reader has actually seen, waits until after the juniors. By then
the reader is on to skills and managing and has stopped looking for it. The Sparrow / Hesselmann paragraph
("some of the alarms didn't hold up") is its natural home.

**Fix.** Move the Kosmyna paragraph to follow Sparrow / Hesselmann in "We've had this worry before", so the
section runs from old alarms through the loudest current one to the pattern. Keep the short-term paragraph
("almost everything here is short-term…") as the limits section, before the advice. This is a move, not a cut:
every word stays.

### 10. The intro opens calculators twice and echoes its own section heading (Impact 5)

**Passage:** intro: "We've heard this worry before. In the 1980s it was calculators… Before that it was writing
itself." Then the section "## We've had this worry before", which opens writing and calculators again.

**Problem.** The reader gets calculators-then-writing in the intro and writing-then-calculators a page later,
under a heading that repeats the intro's sentence almost word for word. Meanwhile the frame, the essay's main
idea, waits for paragraph three. The intro paragraph also makes two promises ("I'll show where" and the
weakest-evidence line).

**Fix.** Compress the intro's calculator paragraph to the turn it needs: "We've heard this worry before, about
calculators and about writing itself, and it has something real in it. But 'it makes you stupid' is too broad
to be useful." The weakest-evidence promise then gets paid early (finding 9). The frame arrives one paragraph
sooner, and the section heading no longer reads as a repeat.

### 11. The close restates the position and opens on the foil (Impact 5)

**Passage:** "Handing work to AI won't make you stupid, any more than calculators did. It's closer to a
promotion…" and last sentence: "Choose on purpose which skills to keep, starting with the ones you need in order
to judge the work, and keep learning them on purpose."

**Problem.** The rule's wording matches the position's, which is right: this is the one rule, stated one way. But
the whole close is the position again with "promotion" added, so the reader gains nothing from the ending
except the reminder. In 06, the close turns ("So the different answer was never the reason to stop trusting it.
It's the first check you get."); here it doesn't. It also opens on calculators, the foil. Whether "won't make
you stupid, any more than calculators did" is earned is Miriam's and Vera's call (flag 7). The craft point is
that the close's first words go to the comparison the essay spent a section complicating.

**Fix.** Open on the frame ("Handing work to AI is closer to a promotion than a decline"), and add one turning
sentence before the rule, built from material already on the page. The strongest candidate is step 3's "That's
the skill that's easiest to let go, and the one you can least afford to", or the "done" moment's "What caught it
was knowing what the work should have produced." Then the rule.

### 12. Title: keep the frame; "Using AI" is broader than the essay (Impact 5; flag 8)

**Passage:** "Using AI is like moving into management. Choose the skills you keep."

**Verdict.** The frame belongs in the title, and "Choose the skills you keep" is good: it's the rule in five
words, and it fits the series' two-sentence imperative form ("Ask the AI twice. Believe the disagreement."
"Don't ask how smart an AI agent is. Ask what it can touch."). The weak word is "Using". The position opens
"handing your work to AI", the close opens "Handing work to AI", and the essay is about handing over tasks, not
asking a chatbot for a recipe. "Using AI" invites the reader who only does the latter to object at the
headline. Leyden & Scur do say "the use of AI", but the essay doesn't need the title to quote them.

**Fix.** "Handing work to AI is like moving into management. Choose the skills you keep." Same shape, and the
first words now match the position's and the close's.

On the Wiles risk: the title says *you* move into management, not "AI is your team", so it doesn't itself
invite the employee reading. Finding 5's sentence closes the gap inside the essay.

### 13. Smaller items (Impact 2–4)

- **"most of us" / "more of us" (4; route to Miriam).** The position: "most of us are making that move". The
  intro: "more of us go from doing the work to directing it". Two sizes of claim three paragraphs apart. Pick
  the one the evidence carries and use it in both.
- **Steps 2 and 4 read as one step (4).** "Keep doing what you're still learning" and "Keep learning on
  purpose." Step 4 means "set time to learn new things in the skills you've kept, not just practice them".
  Retitle it so the bold line says that: "Set time aside to learn, not just to practice."
- **The next-essay pointer mid-body (3).** "Who answers for what an AI does is the subject of the next essay."
  It closes a body section on a teaser. Move it to the end of "Where I land" or to the Notes.
- **"The question this series keeps asking applies here: compared to what?" (3).** That's the third essay
  running to open its comparison section with this formula (07 l.287 is nearly identical). Vary it, or just ask
  the question.
- **"on purpose … on purpose" in the rule (2).** "Choose on purpose … and keep learning them on purpose." Read
  aloud, the second one sounds like an echo. It's Bart's rule, and its wording matches the position, so I'd
  change it only in both places together, if at all. Flagging it for his ear, not asking for the change.
- **"Aviation saw it first." (2).** It introduces a 2014 study, right after a 2025 one. The claim is true of
  Bainbridge (1983), in the next paragraph. Either lead with Bainbridge or say "Aviation met it earlier".

## Flag verdicts

1. **Length.** Every section earns its place. Nothing should be cut for length, and the essay needs *more*
   in three places: the management picture (finding 1), the "manager without an employee" sentence (finding 5),
   and the advice bridge (finding 6). The only cuts are the tag sentences (finding 2) and the mid-body teaser,
   and the reason for each is that it shouldn't be in the essay at any length, not that it's long. The reorder
   (findings 8 and 9) costs no words.
2. **The management frame.** It carries the position, the first trap, the juniors question, and the close. It's
   bolted on through the history, both "What fades" sections, and the second trap: tag sentences at the seams,
   and the skeleton of the earlier draft underneath. The "two traps" read clearly as the familiar traps of
   managing in their *headings* and in the first trap's opening. The second trap's body never shows the manager
   losing touch; Lee et al.'s "their work became more like a manager's" is its only real management beat. Fix
   with findings 1, 2, 6, and 7. Not a rebuild.
3. **Specialists (structural side only).** As placed, it pulls against the frame for most of the essay. Moved
   next to its pay-off, it supports it (finding 3).
4. **Where the comparison needs care.** It's honest, and it doesn't undercut the title. Opened with its
   direction (every break puts more of the judging on you), it becomes the title's best support (finding 4).
5–7. Not my axis. On 7, the craft point is in finding 11.
8. **The title.** Keep it, with "Handing work to AI" for "Using AI" (finding 12).

## Best and weakest

**Best:** "Reading an AI's answer is the rereading. Working it out is the test." Two short sentences that turn a
research finding into something the reader can do, and they set up step 5 without naming it. Close behind:
"In management terms: what happens to people who are asked to direct the work before they've ever done it?",
which is the frame earning its keep. And "They kept the tool and scheduled the practice."

**Weakest:** "Staying close to the work takes effort, for a manager and for anyone using AI." It has rhythm and a
link but no content, and it replaced a line that had both (finding 2).

## Verdict

**Ready after fixes.** The position, the evidence, and the rule are sound. The frame is the right one and needs
finishing, not replacing: one first-hand management picture from Bart, the four tag sentences out, the
specialists and the "done" moment moved to where they do their work, and two short additive sentences
(findings 5 and 6). The reorder (findings 8 and 9) is a move of existing paragraphs. None of it needs a new
source. If Bart can't supply finding 1's picture before Tuesday, the essay still works with findings 2–7 done,
but the frame will rest on the reader's recognition alone.
