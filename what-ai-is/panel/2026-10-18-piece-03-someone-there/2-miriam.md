# Revision plan for piece 03 ("Something is there. Very probably no one."), ranked by impact

**Diagnosis.** The title is the strongest thing in the piece. The case for not claiming consciousness, and for the four rules on how a product should present itself, holds up. The piece undermines both with three big words it hasn't earned: "colleague", "no one", and "very probably". It also treats functionalism as the ground everyone stands on, which it isn't.

**1. The colleague claim rests on Roomba-level agency (Nadia, Rhea O1; Impact 9).**
- **Current words:** "So the interlocutor is a real agent in the interpretive sense, whatever the truth about experience." Also, under Where I land: "**Working with an AI assistant as a colleague isn't a pretence.** On Chalmers's account there is a real agent there…"
- **Problem:** What the claim actually rests on is accurate interpretability. The piece itself says a Roomba has that. The conclusion swaps in a much larger meaning. It also goes beyond piece 01, which allows colleague-reading only for prediction.
- **Fix, in the bullet:** "So there is something to talk *to*: its behavior is accurately described by what it seems to believe and want. That bar is low, and a Roomba clears it. An assistant's apparent beliefs and aims are far richer, rich enough to argue with."
- **Fix, Where I land:** "Treating it as a colleague is a working stance, not a pretence and not a claim about what it is. Reading its apparent aims as you would a colleague's helps predict what it will do and where to check it ([the yardstick](../../positions/01-the-yardstick.md)). It doesn't make it a colleague in the full sense: someone who keeps commitments and answers for them."

**2. "No one" is undefined and "very probably" is one philosopher's 2023 number (Rhea O2; Impact 9).**
- **Current words:** "It is very probably not anyone, not yet" and "nobody can yet check from the inside or the outside."
- **Fix:** add after the position paragraph: "By 'no one' I mean nobody experiencing anything: nothing it is like to be it. That is a different question from whether it has a lasting self or counts as a person."
- **Fix,** in the "Today" section: "'Very probably' is Chalmers's judgement, which I share, not a measurement. His numbers are from 2023. In May 2026 he still said 'most likely not', but gave no new figure. Seth would say 'probably' understates the odds against."
- **Fix:** replace "nobody can yet check…" with "There is no agreed test yet, though some evidence bears on it: the indicator studies check systems against what theories of consciousness say matters."

**3. Functionalism is framed as neutral ground (Anselm, the series ruling; Impact 8).**
- **Current words:** "His conclusion: 'What matters is how neurons or silicon chips are hooked up…'"
- **Fix:** First supply the step Nadia had to fill in herself: "Chalmers finds both implausible, so the experience must stay." Then add: "This is the contested step. If experience depends on being alive, on a body keeping itself going, then copying the organization copies the behavior and may leave the experience behind. Seth argues this, and it is a serious view, not a fringe one."
- **Fix, opening:** "My position, which is largely David Chalmers's, and one serious view among several:"
- **Anselm's misrepresentation point, answered in the presentation section:** "For the biological camp, the four rules follow from what it is. For Chalmers's camp, they follow from what we don't know. The reasons differ, but the rules are the same." That answers him and keeps the claim that the two questions can be separated.

**4. Model versus thread (Nadia; consistency with 02; Impact 8).**
- **Current words:** "not the model, which is one abstract object shared by everyone, and not the server. It is the *thread*…"
- **Fix:** "not the model, the engine everyone's conversations run on ([see the learning piece](../../positions/02-it-learns.md)), and not any one computer. It is the *thread*: your conversation, carried forward by what it holds and remembers. On his view, a new chat with no memory is a new thread. With memory shared across chats, he expects interlocutors to be 'individuated mainly by user', which in practice means one per person. So the thread is what 02's 'it', the whole system, amounts to for you."
- Both points are in source §1.4 and §4.3. Make the same change in 00, lines 84–85.

**5. Searle, Tononi and Koch compressed into one position (Rhea; Impact 7).**
- **Current words:** "**Searle**, and **Tononi and Koch**, reject the idea that running the right program could make a mind."
- **Fix:** "**The philosopher John Searle** argues that running a program is never enough by itself for understanding; a machine would need the right physical causal powers. **The neuroscientists Giulio Tononi and Christof Koch** tie consciousness to a system's physical causal structure, not its software. On their theory a differently built machine could be conscious, but an ordinary computer would not be, whatever it ran."
- Send that sentence to `/cite-check`.

**6. The survey turned into inevitability (Rhea O5, Nadia; Impact 7).**
