**Rosetta: plain-language pass, piece 03 ("Something is there. Very probably no one.")**

**Verdict:** The piece is close to ready to read aloud. The argument holds together, and most of its technical terms are explained where they first appear. What remains are eight terms a listener won't know, three unclear "it"/"he" references, two lists the listener can't count as they hear them, and two British spellings. None of the edits below changes a claim, number, quote, citation or the last line.

**Whole-piece pass, read cold as a listener:**
1. **Kinds of number:** one. Every figure is a percentage (under 10, 25 or more, 67), and each one comes with a plain caveat.
2. **Places the reader is sent elsewhere:** the links to pieces 01, 02 and 04. They are optional, and the argument stands without them.
3. **Terms that are precise to a researcher but empty to a lay reader:** "metaphysics", "interlocutor" and "credences" are the worst. They are covered in edits 1, 7, 9 and 10.

**Edits, highest impact first**

1. "Where memory is shared across chats, he expects interlocutors to be "individuated mainly by user", in practice one per person." -> "Where memory is shared across chats, he expects one conversation partner per person: in his words, "individuated mainly by user"."
   *Why:* "Interlocutor" and "individuated" come back to back. Putting the plain meaning first lets the quote land. **Impact 7**

2. "Evidence from the consciousness-indicator studies, which check systems against what theories of consciousness say matters, that current systems meet those markers" -> "Evidence from studies that check AI systems against the features theories of consciousness say matter, showing that current systems have those features"
   *Why:* As written, the jargon label comes first and the main claim arrives 25 words later. **Impact 7**

3. "It doesn't make it a colleague in the full sense" -> "That stance doesn't make the assistant a colleague in the full sense"
   *Why:* Two "it"s pointing at different things (the stance and the assistant), one after the other. **Impact 6**

4. "He also calls unified agency as "maybe the deepest" obstacle" -> "He also names unified agency as "maybe the deepest" obstacle"
   *Why:* "Calls ... as" is a grammar slip, and it is audible. **Impact 6**

5. "In May 2026 he gave no new figures, but said he was "inclined to think the current language models are not conscious", and that the case for future models being conscious "is not that easy to rebut"." -> "In May 2026 he gave no new figures. He said he was "inclined to think the current language models are not conscious". He also said the case for future models being conscious "is not that easy to rebut"."
   *Why:* At 40-plus words with two quotes nested inside, the sentence is too long to follow by ear. **Impact 6**

6. "What makes the trajectory worth watching is his roadmap of what would raise the odds:" -> "What makes the trajectory worth watching is his roadmap of five things that would raise the odds:"
   *Why:* Text-to-speech runs list items together. Giving the count up front lets the listener keep track. **Impact 5**

7. "Skeptics, centrists and the open-minded disagree about the metaphysics." -> "Skeptics, centrists and the open-minded disagree about what these systems really are."
   *Why:* "Metaphysics" is a jargon word, and the replacement is the plain-language claim it stands for. **Impact 6**

8. "a product should not:" -> "a product should not do four things:"
   *Why:* Same reason as edit 6. **Impact 4**

9. "The philosopher Jonathan Birch denies the persistent interlocutor:" -> "The philosopher Jonathan Birch denies that there is a lasting someone you are talking to:"
   *Why:* "Interlocutor" is a jargon word. The plain wording also sets up his quote, "There is no friend". **Impact 6**

10. "his own credences ran somewhat higher on both counts" -> "his own estimates ran somewhat higher on both counts"
    *Why:* "Credences" is a philosopher's term, and these words are a paraphrase, not a quote. **Impact 5**

11. "He calls it a *thread* (a virtual instance, when only one model is involved)." -> "He calls it a thread." Move "when only one model is involved, his term is a virtual instance" to a note.
    *Why:* Read aloud, the bracketed aside becomes a second, unexplained term in the middle of a definition, and the voice also drops the italics. **Impact 5**

12. "For the biological camp, those rules follow from what it is." -> "For the biological camp, those rules follow from what the system is."
    *Why:* "It" has no clear referent here. The nearest candidates are "the rules" and "a product". **Impact 5**

13. "on their integrated information theory," -> "on their own theory of consciousness, called integrated information theory,"
    *Why:* Tells the listener what kind of thing the name refers to. **Impact 4**

14. Spelling. "his judgement" -> "his judgment", and "not a pretence" -> "not a pretense".
    *Why:* The series uses American spelling. **Impact 3**

**Left as they are, on purpose**
- "Hard problem" is explained right after the colon.
- "Quasi" is defined straight away, and the definition still works if the quote marks are dropped.
- "Near those axes" is minor. "Near those features" would be a little plainer.
- The bold labels ("What kind of thing", "Which thing") still read well aloud without the bold.

**What language can't fix:** the "What you're actually talking to" section asks the listener to hold three candidates at once (the model, the computer, the thread) plus a fourth they are told to set aside (the persona). Edits 1 and 11 make that easier, but the load is part of the argument itself. If it doesn't play well on the podcast, a short spoken recap ("so: not the model, not the machine, the conversation") would help. That is the author's call.
