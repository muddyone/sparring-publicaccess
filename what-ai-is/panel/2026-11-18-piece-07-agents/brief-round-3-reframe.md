## 1. What is new: the reframe

Bart, reading the final draft on 11-20: *"the title rings awkward because most people don't think a smarter AI
would be a safer one. The opposite is true. Most people think the smarter AI gets, the more dangerous they become.
So, while our content is relevant and interesting, it is couched backwards."*

The old title was "A smarter agent isn't a safer one. Ask what it can touch." The old position argued against
"smarter = safer". The revision (diff: `git show 243bb509`) changes:

- **Title:** "Worried AI agents are getting too smart? Ask what they can touch." (working title; judge it)
- **Position paragraph:** opens on the reader's worry (smarter = more dangerous), says there's something to it
  but it watches the wrong dial, keeps the access/capability/oversight structure, adds "a less capable one isn't
  the answer either" and "you don't control how smart the next model is; you do control what it can touch".
- **Opening paragraph:** sets the industry pitch and the reader's worry side by side; both are about capability.
- **Section retitled** "What being smarter changes" (was "A smarter model isn't a safer one"): the worry and the
  industry's hope meet; Debenedetti 2024 is read as "the worry, half right" (the smarter model was more dangerous
  because it followed someone else's orders better, not because it had plans of its own); Lynch 2025 is "the
  closest the evidence comes to the worry in its usual form".
- **"Neither 'use a smarter model' nor 'use a less capable one'"** replaces the one-sided line.
- **Where I land, What this doesn't claim, What would change my mind:** each gains a line on the worry.
