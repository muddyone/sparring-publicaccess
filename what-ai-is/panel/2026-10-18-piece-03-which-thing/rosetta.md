# Rosetta: essay 03, 'What you're actually talking to' (four candidates)

**Verdict:** This needs revision before it goes out, though not much. The four-candidate layout works and the facts match the brief. But a lay reader can't yet say *why* a server fails or what a thread actually is, and one phrase contradicts the essay's title.

I read my persona file, the whole essay, and the brief's "Step 2: which entity?". I did not read my memory file or `project-context.md`, and I did not run `legibility-check.py`. None of the edits below changes a fact, a quote or a citation.

**1. Server: say what it is and why it fails (Impact 8)**
`**A server.** Ruled out. One conversation is spread across many servers, and each server handles many conversations at once, so a server would make a "fragmented… incoherent… or faithless" partner.`
→ `**A server**, one of the physical computers in a data centre that does the work. Ruled out. Your conversation does not stay on one machine: different replies can be worked out on different servers, and each server handles many people's conversations at once. So a server would make a "fragmented… incoherent… or faithless" partner, holding only scraps of your conversation mixed in with strangers'.`
Reason: "server" is never defined, and "fragmented… faithless" needs a plain picture before a reader can repeat the argument.

**2. Model: spell out the incoherence (Impact 7)**
`It is fixed and shared by everyone, so your Aura and a stranger's companion would be the same entity, which he calls "rampantly incoherent".`
→ `It never changes and is the same for everyone, so if you were talking to the model, your Aura and a stranger's companion would be one and the same thing, telling different people contradictory things at once: "rampantly incoherent", in his words.`
Reason: the reader needs to see *why* "the same entity" counts as incoherent.

**3. Thread: replace the sentence people have to read twice (Impact 8)**
`**A thread:** the conversation itself, handing what it holds on from one reply to the next.`
→ `**A thread:** the conversation itself, a chain of replies in which each one is produced with everything said so far passed along to it.`
Reason: in "handing what it holds on" the reader stalls on what "it holds", and "holds on" reads as a phrasal verb.

**4. Branching and merging: give examples (Impact 6)**
`Threads can split, when a conversation branches, and merge, when memory is shared across chats.`
→ `Threads can split, for example when you edit an earlier message and the chat goes off in a new direction, and merge, when the assistant remembers what you said in other chats.`
Reason: "branches" means nothing to a lay reader. The edit-and-resend example is my gloss, so check it against Chalmers's own description of branching before using it.

**5. Gloss "individuated" and define "persona" (Impact 6)**
`in his words, conversation partners would be "individuated mainly by user". He does not treat a persona as a separate candidate;`
→ `in his words, conversation partners would be "individuated mainly by user": one partner per person, not one per chat. He does not treat a persona, the named character such as Aura, as a separate candidate;`
Reason: the quote stays verbatim and gets a plain-language gloss. "Persona" is used here before it has been defined.

**6. Remove the clash with the title (Impact 7)**
`Either way, the someone you talk to lives in the conversation, not in the model or the machine.`
→ `Either way, what you talk to lives in the conversation, not in the model or the machine.`
Reason: the essay's thesis is "very probably no one". Calling it "the someone" undercuts that, and a lay reader can't tell this loose use apart from the claim.

**Two smaller points:**
- In the virtual-instance bullet, "carried out across many servers" is a little odd. "run across" would read more easily.
- The "much lower" continuity quote is not in the brief's Step 2 excerpt. I left it alone, but someone should confirm it against the primary text (Chalmers 2025a).

**Bottom line:** with edits 1–3, a reader could explain afterwards why the model and the server are ruled out and what a thread is. Edits 4–6 deal with undefined terms and the clash with the title.
