# Julian: essay 03, 'What you're actually talking to' (four candidates)

**Verdict:** The section earns most of its new length. The four bullets read cleanly, and "Either way, the someone you talk to lives in the conversation" is the right landing. The problem is what comes after it: two more paragraphs keep going past that line, and the closing unified-agency paragraph repeats a point the roadmap list already made. The design point, which is the reason a builder cares about any of this, ends up buried in the middle.

**Edits**

1. "His answer has two parts, and it is the part of his view I find most useful." → "His answer has two parts, and it is the piece of his view I find most useful."
   *Reason:* "parts" and "part" in one sentence point at two different things, and the reader trips on it.

2. "one running copy of the model, carried out across many servers, the way" → "one running copy of the model, run across many servers, the way"
   *Reason:* "carried out" is a word for tasks, not copies. "Run" matches the brief's "realised by a series of hardware instances" without a technical term.

3. "The thread covers every case, so this essay uses it." → "The thread covers every case, so it is the answer I use."
   *Reason:* "this essay uses it" promises a term the essay never uses again. Making it first person keeps the claim honest and the rhythm short.

4. Move "He does not treat a persona as a separate candidate; it is part of the character of the thread that carries it." up to just after "...not in the model or the machine.", reworded as: "Aura, then, is not a separate thing: Chalmers treats a persona as part of the character of the thread that carries it."
   *Reason:* where it sits now, it cuts in between the split/merge facts and the design point they lead to. Moved up, it closes the loop on the Aura opening.

5. "On my reading, that makes how many there are partly a design choice: shared memory tends towards one per person, and sealed-off conversations make one each." → "On my reading, that makes the number of someones partly a design choice: shared memory tends towards one per person, and sealed-off conversations make one per chat."
   *Reason:* "how many there are" has nothing clear to refer back to, and "one each" makes the reader work out each of what. This is the line a builder will remember, so it should be the section's last beat and should land hard.

6. Move the final paragraph ("He also names unified agency as 'maybe the deepest' obstacle... counts against anyone being home.") out of this section, to sit right after the roadmap bullets, before "That reads like a product roadmap."
   *Reason:* it repeats the fifth roadmap bullet ("one consistent agent, not many characters"), and it turns back to consciousness after the section has moved on to which thing you are talking to. Next to the roadmap it strengthens that list. Here, it buries edit 5.

**Considered and left alone**
- The five requirements followed by "Roughly, ..." and five glosses. It is a doubled list, but lay readers need the glosses, and "He tries four candidates." gives a clean short beat after them.
- The repeated "Ruled out." in the first two bullets. The parallel lets the reader see the elimination happening, and leaving it off the last two bullets correctly signals that those two survive.

All facts, quotes and citations are unchanged. Edits 2 and 4 stay within the brief's "Step 2" wording. I couldn't find the "much lower" continuity quote in the Step 2 excerpt, so I left it exactly as written.
