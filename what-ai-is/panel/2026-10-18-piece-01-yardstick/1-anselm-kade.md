# Outside review: Anselm Kade

_Substrate: gemini/gemini-3.1-pro-preview. Piece: 01y-for-outside.md. Finish: stop._

### VERDICT

The central argument fails because it rests on a purely functionalist view of cognition, stripping "roles" of the biological embodiment and existential stakes that make human judgment possible. By defining roles merely as information-processing tasks, the piece begs the question, creating a false equivalence between a human intern who understands the world through lived experience and a statistical model that simply correlates text. 

### OBJECTIONS

**1. The functionalist fallacy of substrate-neutral "roles"**
*Quote:* "The better question isn't 'is it reliable software?' It's 'how does it compare with whoever would otherwise fill this role...'"
*Objection:* The piece assumes cognitive and social roles are substrate-neutral and defined entirely by informational inputs and outputs.
*Strongest form:* From a biologically-grounded perspective, roles like "advising" and "deciding" are not mere text-generation tasks; they are acts of sense-making rooted in physical vulnerability, caring, and embeddedness in a shared human world. An intern exercises judgment because they have a physical body, social stakes, and an existential fear of failure. A language model possesses none of this. By defining a "role" purely by its functional output, the piece begs the question, ignoring that human judgment relies on a background of embodied common sense that algorithms lack entirely. 
*Citation:* Dreyfus, H. L. (1992). *What Computers Still Can't Do: A Critique of Artificial Reason*. MIT Press.
*Change:* Concede that "role" here is used strictly as a metaphor for task-output, and heavily qualify that AI fundamentally lacks the embodied stakes and caring that constitute human judgment.

**2. Conflating predictive fictions with genuine agency**
*Quote:* "Daniel Dennett's intentional stance says we predict complex systems by treating them as agents with aims... an apparent aim should be responded to the way we'd respond to a colleague's."
*Objection:* The argument leaps from a pragmatic, user-interface strategy to a social and cognitive equivalence.
*Strongest form:* The intentional stance is merely a heuristic for the observer, not proof of intrinsic agency in the system. Enactive cognitive science demonstrates that genuine aims require a precarious, self-producing biological organization (autopoiesis) that must act to maintain itself. An AI has no self to maintain; it cares about nothing. Treating a predictive illusion as a basis for responding "the way we'd respond to a colleague" conflates a useful fiction with reality, demanding we grant social trust to an entity utterly devoid of intrinsic intentionality. 
*Citation:* Thompson, E. (2007). *Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind*. Harvard University Press.
*Change:* Cut the comparison to "a colleague's" aims. Acknowledge that the intentional stance is a fictional overlay we project onto the machine, not a justification for treating it as a social agent.

**3. The false dichotomy of the "intern" versus the "calculator"**
*Quote:* "It judges a system that does judgment work by the standards of a calculator. [...] Generative AI increasingly works in the intern's role..."
*Objection:* The argument relies on a false dichotomy that forces the reader to accept the anthropomorphic "intern" framing simply because AI is more complex than a basic calculator.
*Strongest form:* There is a vast excluded middle between a pocket calculator and a human intern. A system can be highly complex, probabilistic, and inconsistent without possessing the cognitive status of an entity doing "judgment work." Critics of the extended-mind thesis point out that coupling a human to a highly complex, non-cognitive tool does not render the tool itself cognitive. The AI is not a colleague; it is a complex, probabilistic calculator. The "intern" analogy illegitimately smuggles in human agency to excuse software unreliability.
*Citation:* Adams, F., & Aizawa, K. (2008). *The Bounds of Cognition*. Blackwell.
*Change:* Cut the phrase "judgment work" and concede that the "intern" framing is a rhetorical device, replacing it with the admission that generative AI is a highly complex probabilistic tool, not a junior agent.

**4. Equivocating on the nature of "opacity"**
*Quote:* "Philosophers Zerilli, Knott, Maclaurin and Gavaghan argue that AI is held to 'an unrealistically high standard' of transparency, partly because we overestimate how transparent human decisions are..."
*Objection:* The piece conflates human psychological opacity with algorithmic, mathematical opacity.
*Strongest form:* A human expert's reasoning may be opaque to introspection, but it is grounded in a shared physical and social reality. We can cross-examine a human, gauge their physical hesitations, read their sincerity, and rely on their shared biological embodiment to contextualize their "opaque" intuition. Algorithmic opacity is entirely alien; it stems from billions of statistical weights mapping tokens in high-dimensional space without any grounding in a lived world. Equating the two falsely implies they carry the same type of risk.
*Citation:* No citation.
*Change:* Add the qualification that human opacity is mitigated by shared embodied contexts and biological common sense, whereas AI opacity is utterly unmoored from human reality.

### MISREPRESENTATIONS

*   **Critics of AI transparency:** The piece misrepresents critics by suggesting they simply "overestimate how transparent human decisions are." Critics demand AI transparency not because they think humans are perfectly logical calculators, but precisely because AI lacks the shared biological grounding, physical vulnerability, and social accountability that naturally check and balance opaque human intuition.
*   **Accountability and System Boundaries:** By claiming that a "named person answers for its use" resolves accountability, the piece misrepresents critics of human-machine functionalism. Critics do not accept that assigning a human "manager" to a black-box system magically turns an unpredictable algorithmic output into a responsible socio-technical unit. It glosses over the fact that the human cannot genuinely oversee reasoning they cannot access.

### WHAT THE PIECE GETS RIGHT THAT YOUR SIDE SHOULD CONCEDE

The piece correctly identifies that human decision-making is deeply flawed, inconsistent, and easily warped by social dynamics (like sycophancy), making an idealized standard of human perfection a dishonest baseline for critique. Furthermore, its recognition that current AI intrinsically lacks curiosity—an embodied, self-motivated drive to explore—is an accurate and necessary concession.
