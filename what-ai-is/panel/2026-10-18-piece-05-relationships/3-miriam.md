**Miriam Vale: revision plan for piece 05 ("We don't ask whether friendship is safe. We ask which ones are.")**

**Diagnosis.** The central idea holds: harms we know only as cases can't tell us how often harm happens, and the real question is which designs are safe. But by the end the piece claims more than it asked the reader to accept at the start. Its tone also slips right next to children's deaths. And it reaches its best line three sections before it ends.

**1. Claim growth (Nadia; Rhea 1, 6). Impact 10.**
- **Current (opening):** "I expect them to show a different profile of risk from human relationships, not a worse one, alongside large benefits."
- **Current (end):** "no worse than the comparable human risks, and likely better, while bringing benefits in access and productivity on the scale of the car or electricity in the home."
- **Fix (opening):** "When those numbers arrive, I expect a companion designed against the known harms to prove no more harmful than the human relationship it stands in for, in the same people. Because one model reaches millions at once, it should be held to a higher bar than one friend, not the same bar. That expectation is mine, and the evidence doesn't settle it."
- At the end, cut "likely better" and the car and electricity comparison. Restate exactly the opening claim (see 11).
- **Current:** "the missing numbers could as easily argue for these systems as against them."
- **Fix:** "The missing numbers could cut either way. I've said which way I expect, and that it is only an expectation."
- **Knock-on in 00:** change "can be designed out" to "can be designed against".

**2. Ines's three must-fix sentences. Impact 9.** Use her restatements word for word.
- **Raine:** "As of October 2026 the case was ongoing, and no court had ruled on its claims."
- **Belgium:** "A Belgian man in his thirties died by suicide in 2023. Already anxious about the climate, he had spent six weeks confiding in a Chai chatbot. His widow told *La Libre* that without those conversations he would still be alive." Add Incident Database report 2867 or 3002 as a source for his age.
- **Cheng:** "Even one conversation with a sycophantic AI made people less willing to repair a conflict and more certain they were right, and they trusted and preferred it more."

**3. Tone next to the deaths (Nadia; Rhea on Dateline). Impact 8.**
- **Current:** "A child's death is vivid, and a rate is dry. But the error is in letting the stories stand in for the analysis."
  **Fix:** "It is right that these deaths lead the conversation. They show what is at stake. The mistake would be to let them answer a question they can't: how often."
- **Current:** "Cars kill people. Gas stoves cause fires. We don't conclude that driving or cooking is unsafe, because we weigh…"
  **Fix:** "With everything else we live with, from medicines to cars, we weigh the harm against how often it happens and against what it gives us."
- **Current:** the Friends bullet, from "Watch _Dateline_" through "what we have is cases."
  **Fix:** "**Friends.** A person drawn into one close relationship that cuts them off from everyone else and leads them somewhere dark is an old human story, and nobody counts those either. On both sides, what we have is cases."
- **Current heading:** "AI is getting better. Are people?"
  **Fix:** "The harms come from product choices, and products can change"

**4. What "it" is, and "persona" (Nadia). Impact 8.** Add a scope line straight after the position paragraph:

Therapy tools built for the role are a different product, and I keep them separate. AI at work has [its own piece](../../positions/04-personality.md). Wider questions, such as environmental cost, are outside this piece."

**5. The author's stake, up front (Nadia). Impact 7.** Put this after the scope line:

**6. Uneven causal caution (Rhea 2). Impact 7.** The piece treats the two sides of the comparison differently.

  **Fix:** "That counts people who show signs of crisis while using it."

  **Fix:** "also leaves a minority worse off, often unseen. How much of that the therapy caused is as hard to untangle as it is for chatbots."

**7. "Design" as an explanation nobody can disprove (Rhea 3). Impact 7.**

**9. Correctability, stated in the piece itself (f; Rhea 7). Impact 6.**

**10. Memory, stated in the piece itself (f). Impact 5.**

**11. Structure and ending (g). Impact 8.**
- Cut the section "What this means for personality at work" (see 12).
- Move "What's missing" and "What would change my mind" ahead of a new closing section, "Where I land", which replaces "Where my position goes beyond the evidence".
- Move the friendship paragraph into it.
- **Where I land:** "The harms are real, and some are tragic. We know them as cases, and cases can't tell us how often harm happens, or how that compares with the human relationships these systems stand in for. The evidence supports two things: these relationships can do real harm, and tools designed for the role have delivered measured benefit. I expect more: that a companion designed against the known harms will prove no more harmful than the human relationship it stands in for, held to the higher bar its reach demands. That is a claim to test, not a finding.

  We don't ask whether friendship is safe. We know some friendships are good for us and some do terrible damage, and we ask which kind a given one is. Ask the same of AI companions."
- **Last line:** "Not whether they are safe, but which ones are. And with a companion, which one is a choice someone made."

**Verdict.** Once items 1, 2 and 11 are done, the piece makes one claim sized to the evidence and ends on its best line. The test it wins on is honesty: it should end by claiming exactly what it asked the reader to accept at the start.
