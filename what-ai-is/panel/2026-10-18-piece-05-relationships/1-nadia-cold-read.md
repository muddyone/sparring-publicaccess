_Substrate: Claude (subagent as Nadia Okonkwo), general-reader cold read. Piece 05._

**Nadia Okonkwo, cold read of piece 05 (relationships)**

**1. What it argues, and what "it" is.** It argues that the harms of AI relationships are real but known only as individual stories, so we can't yet say these relationships are more dangerous than human ones. The writer expects they aren't, and says the real question is which designs are safe. "It" is supposed to be chatbots people form relationships with: companion apps like Character.AI and Replika. But it keeps sliding into ChatGPT and, near the end, into work assistants.

**2. Did I know what the subject was?** Mostly, but it got muddy early. The position opens with "a relationship with an AI persona," and I didn't know what a persona was. A character? Any chatbot? The first case fits (a Character.AI character). Then Adam Raine is a ChatGPT case, and I wondered whether that was a "relationship" or a kid using a general tool. The piece half-admits this later ("general companion apps and general AI assistants. They aren't the same products"). By then I had been reading every case as the same thing. "What this means for personality at work" felt like a different essay.

**3. Where I slowed down, stopped believing it, or felt the tone was off.**
- On tone, if I knew a family affected: "A child's death is vivid, and a rate is dry." It's accurate, but "vivid" made me flinch. "Watch _Dateline_, _20/20_ or _48 Hours_ for a few evenings" felt glib right after deaths of children, even though the writer then calls it judgement, not measurement. The heading "AI is getting better. Are people?" reads as a jab. Cars and gas stoves come too soon after the deaths.
- Where I stopped believing it: the opening says it expects "a different profile of risk... not a worse one." The end says "no worse... and likely better," with benefits "on the scale of the car or electricity in the home." That is a much bigger claim than the one I agreed to at the top. It also sits badly next to "the missing numbers could as easily argue for these systems as against them," since the writer clearly expects them to argue for.
- "In my own work building these systems." Who are you? A builder arguing builders' products are fine needs to tell me that at the top, not in passing.
- Where I slowed down: the therapist numbers ("told that 8%... flagged only 3 of 550") and the Folk & Dunn paragraph. Both took two reads, which is two more than I give.
- What worked: the car in Drive with no one in it. That clicked immediately and changed how I read the studies.
- One small thing: the notes say "Not yet reviewed by a panel." That made me trust it a little less, and I wondered whether it was out of date.

**4. Does it end on a finished beat?** No, it drifts. It ends on a bulleted "what would change my mind" list and then the notes. That's honest, but it isn't a landing. The strongest closing beat is earlier, under the AI-getting-better heading: "We don't ask whether friendship is safe... not whether they are safe, but which designs are." That's the line that stuck, and it's also the title. The piece peaks there and keeps going for three more sections.

**5. What I'd be missing if handed only this piece.**
- What "persona" means.
- Who the writer is, and what "my own tools" are.
- What "the correctability point from the yardstick piece" says. It's leaned on as if I already know it.
- Why memory is "what turns a model into a relationship." That is asserted by pointing to other pieces.
- The most practical gap: a piece that names teen suicides has no crisis line or "if this is you" note anywhere. If I'd forwarded it to a friend who'd lost someone, that's the first thing I'd notice was missing.
