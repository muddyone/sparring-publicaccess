# Nadia Okonkwo's cold read of "It learns. The better question is what kind of learner it is."

Two notes on how I read it. My persona says no impact scores and no structured reports, but you asked for both, so I've given them and kept the voice. And you told me the reader is a general, curious adult, not the AI-governance professional my persona describes, so I read as that person.

## 1. What I think it argues

If an AI remembers what you tell it and acts differently afterwards, it learns, and the real question is how its learning differs from ours. The author thinks the main difference is that it has to write lessons down instead of absorbing them, and he ran a small test suggesting that writing them down can work.

What I took away is mostly "writing it down still counts as learning." "Discover versus receive" did not come through as the open question. "Function, not form" was clear in the opening and then felt more like a debating move than the spine of the piece.

## 2. Does "function, not form" hold from opening to end?

It holds clearly through the nickname test and H.M. It breaks in five places:

- **"Both of us overreached, and in the same way: we each leaned on form."** I lost it here. Gopnik's case, as the piece just described it, is "cultural technology versus truth-seeker," which doesn't sound like a claim about form. Then "Her contrasts mostly dissolve," and three paragraphs later her contrast survives as "a claim about function." Which of her claims were about form? I couldn't say.
- **"A person predicts, acts, lives with the result and adjusts, with something at stake."** This is offered as the real difference. Is having something at stake form or function? The piece doesn't say, right where it most needs to.
- **"Every learner has a profile. Ask what this one's is."** The opening said the open question was discover versus receive. Here it has become facts versus skills, the H.M.-style profile. Those are two different "what kind of learner" questions, and the piece blends them without telling me.
- **The start of "What I'm testing."** "Gopnik's surviving claim," which the author "thinks is right," is followed by three bullets in which newer models mostly catch up. Is her claim still right or not? The thread went slack.
- **"What would change my mind," third bullet.** The contrast is between acting on the world and "learning continuously," but the piece never said continuous learning was the edge. The thread ends on a framing I hadn't been given.

## 3. Where I stumbled

- **The italic header.** "cite-checked synthesis," "panel-reviewed," "the drafting model is an interested party." This is the first thing I saw, and it's lab paperwork. "Drafting model" took a beat before I realised it meant the AI that helped write this, which is the kind of thing being judged. If this header ships, it's the first wall a reader hits. **Impact 7.** Fix: move it to the end, and say in plain words that an AI helped write a piece about whether AI learns.
- **"That is the point of 'what it knows': learning can sit quietly until it's needed. So judging by function is not judging by behavior alone."** I re-read this. I had just taken function to mean "what it does," and now function isn't behavior. The distinction between what it can do and what it does is real but arrives in a rush. **Impact 6.** Fix: one sentence saying function means what it can do, not only what you see it doing.
- **"Otto and his notebook."** Who is Otto? It's dropped in like I should know. **Impact 4.** Fix: one clause saying Otto is a man with memory loss who relies on a notebook.
- **"the upgrades it receives."** Upgrades count as the system learning? That reads as someone else doing the learning for it. See section 5. **Impact 6.**
- **"Her published case is more careful than the two-year-old who outsmarts the best AI."** At first it read as comparing her case to a toddler. **Impact 3.** Fix: "more careful than that soundbite."
- **"draw a circle with no compass; choose a ruler, a round-bottomed teapot or a stove."** Why a teapot? I worked out on the second pass that you trace its round bottom. **Impact 6.** Fix: say the teapot is the right answer and why.
- **"chose the object that would work 85% of the time on average."** I first read it as an object that works 85% of the time. **Impact 5.** Fix: "picked the working object 85% of the time."
- **"On the matching *imitation* question… the models did well."** I didn't see why this matters until much later. **Impact 4.**
- **"Bayesian learners weighing evidence, which is pattern learning over interventions rather than over text."** Two terms I don't have, "Bayesian" and "interventions," in the sentence doing the argument's work. **Impact 5.** Fix: "learning from what happens when they poke at things, not from reading."
- **Table row "Rules over impulses."** I couldn't say what this row is about. **Impact 4.**
- **"For knowledge work… that gap is small. For discovery, it may not be."** Why would discovery need knowledge that can't be written down? The piece says it and moves on. This is a jump. **Impact 6.**
- **"explore less efficiently, with a similar gap."** Similar to what? **Impact 6.**
- **"Adults who explore for themselves get much better at 'needs two', though it still takes them more tests."** Adults, or models? Better than what, and more tests than whom? I re-read this and still guessed. **Impact 6.**
- **"pre-registered."** This is fine for some readers but meaningless to a general one. **Impact 4.** Fix: "wrote down the rules and what would count as success before running it."
- **"neither model carried the rule."** Three models were just named. Which two are "neither," and where did Grok go? **Impact 5.**
- **"That is the learning-profile gap put to a test"** appears twice, at the study setup and again at the result. The repeat read like a copy-paste. **Impact 3.**
- **"If models updated themselves continually without forgetting… the result might look much the same."** How would that change his mind? It sounds like it would support him. **Impact 5.**

## 4. Where the emphasis feels wrong

- **Too much on the study.** The test section is the longest in the piece, for a result the author himself calls one task, two models and ten runs. A position piece turns into a lab report at the end, and that's where I'd stop caring as a general reader.
- **Too much on the 2023 percentages** (85%, 76%, then 81.3% and 88.2%). Numbers about out-of-date models slow the Gopnik section, and what I wanted there was her argument.
- **Too little on defending the definition.** The piece uses the thermostat line to knock down "pattern machine" as too broad, then never asks whether its own definition is too broad as well. See section 5.
- **Too little on discover versus receive.** The opening promises it as the real question, but it gets one bolded sentence in the middle and is then overtaken by the facts-versus-skills profile.
- **H.M. is weighted right.** It's the best part of the piece, clear and earned.

## 5. What I didn't believe

- **The definition is as broad as the thing it mocks.** "Experience caused it, it lasts, it changed what the system knows, it changes what it does later." My phone's contacts app meets that. So does a sticky note on my fridge. If "pattern machine" fails because a thermostat qualifies, "lasting change caused by experience" fails because a notepad qualifies. Moving the unit to "the whole system" doesn't answer this; it's what invites it. This is the first thing I'd say in the comments. **Impact 8.**
- **The second-grader analogy.** A child becomes a third grader through her own experience. The piece itself later admits the model "doesn't change from its own experience between releases," and that each release "learns as a lineage, not as an individual." So the next model isn't the same learner, older. A company trains a new one. The analogy carries more than it can bear. **Impact 7.**
- **Gopnik's surviving claim.** Calling it right, then citing evidence that models mostly catch up and match adults, makes me unsure what the author believes about her. **Impact 6.**
- **The headline result.** The model that helped write the piece wins its own test, 10 out of 10, while the other vendor's model shows nothing beyond chance. The disclosure is there and the caveats are honest, but a skeptical reader will notice. I'd want the replication before this result is the payoff the piece builds to. **Impact 5.**

**Bottom line:** the opening, the nickname test and H.M. are strong and easy to follow. I'd lose the thread at "Both of us overreached," because I can't tell which of Gopnik's claims were about form. If I commented, it would be about the contacts-app objection to the definition.
