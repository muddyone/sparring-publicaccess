# Nadia Okonkwo's cold read of the revised piece 01

I read it all the way through without stopping.

**1. What it argues.** It argues that the useful question about AI learning isn't whether it learns the way we do. It's what it can do with experience, and whether it can find things out for itself rather than just being told. "It" is the whole assistant: the language model plus the memory and notes around it. The model is the engine and the assistant is the car.

**2. Did "it" stay clear?** Yes, from early on. The engine-and-car line did the job, and "It hasn't always been able to learn in use" told me which period he means. It mostly held. It got muddy in three places:

- **The second-grader comparison in "Which thing learns."** It says "she is still learning," but the frozen model isn't. Only the assistant around it is. For a second I thought he was claiming the model keeps learning, and I had to sort that out myself.
- **The same section adds the line of models as a second learner.** So by the middle there are three candidates: the model, the assistant and the line. The car picture had promised me one thing.
- **The study sections switch to "a model."** "Can a rule a model discovers be kept in writing," "at least one model," "Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol." By then he means the assistant taking notes, but the word on the page is "model," the very word he told me not to mean. Small, but I noticed it.

**3. Does the ending land?** Yes. "Where I land" pulls the thread back, and the last line is the one I'll remember:

> "So stop asking whether it learns. It does. Ask whether it can find out what nobody has told it."

It felt finished. The one stretch that drifts is just before it: three study versions, a list of limits, then "what would change my mind." It's honest, but it's a lot of throat-clearing between the result and the landing.

**4. Where I slowed down.** The third study version. Without notes, Claude "usually guessed the rule (8 times in 10)" but "found the right pair only 2 times in 10." I didn't know what "the pair" was. It's mentioned once earlier ("which two blocks it uses") and I'd lost it by then. The next line, "So the notes didn't fix a weakness; Claude already leaned the right way," made me stop. Going from 2 in 10 to 10 in 10 sounds like fixing something to me. I worked out that he means the rule-guessing was already fine and the notes helped it spend its tests better. I only got that on the second pass.

I also believed the result a bit less once I read that Claude, made by the same company as the AI that helped write the piece, was the one that showed the effect, and that this AI sorted the notes itself. He says so openly, which I respect. But it's the line I'd remember if a friend asked whether the study proved anything.
