# Outside review: Rhea Castellan

_Substrate: openai/gpt-6.1-sol. Piece: 01-for-outside.md. Finish: stop._

## VERDICT

The central argument survives only in its modest form: a model-plus-memory system can undergo experience-dependent changes that count as learning. The new conclusion overreaches by treating that classification as an answer to a developmental position that already concerns the quality, efficiency and generality of learning—not whether the learner has a brain or changes its weights.

## OBJECTIONS

### 1. The conclusion reinstates the opponent the body has abandoned

“The case against rests on form: it doesn't learn the way we do.”

“So stop asking whether it learns. It does.”

Your own account of Gopnik’s published argument contradicts this conclusion. You explicitly identify functional disagreements: selecting informative experiments, discovering causal structure and transferring expectations. None requires denying that storing and subsequently using a nickname qualifies as learning.

The strongest objection is that your definition settles a permissive classification question while leaving the substantive developmental comparison open. A system can satisfy that definition and remain a poor causal learner. Conversely, someone can accept your whole-system boundary and external memory while questioning whether the resulting system learns efficiently or generalizes appropriately. Those are not objections to its material or computational form.

Yiu, Kosoy and Gopnik’s paper makes a functional comparison between transmission and innovation, not a requirement that learning involve biological machinery (2023 online, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*). Lucas, Bridgers, Griffiths and Gopnik likewise investigate differences in learning causal forms, not eligibility for the word “learning” (2014, *Cognition*).

**Change:** Cut the claim that “the case against” rests on form, or restrict it to the particular frozen-weights objection you quote. Qualify the closing imperative: your argument establishes that minimal learning occurs, not that asking which learning capacities are present has become unnecessary.

### 2. “Tested with no memory” introduces an unsupported explanation of the earlier results

“Those were 2022 and 2023 models, tested with no memory of past conversations, and newer ones have narrowed the gap (below).”

The juxtaposition invites readers to understand missing conversational memory as a limitation relevant to the reported tool-innovation deficit. You have not established that connection.

The strongest objection is methodological: these were questions about what a learner could infer from the current problem. Persistent memory would matter if previous encounters supplied useful experience, but then those encounters become part of the experimental treatment. Giving one participant relevant accumulated experience and another only the test question changes the comparison; it does not merely repair a defective memory system.

Even granting that every tested model lacked persistent conversational memory, that fact does not explain its answer to the teapot problem. In-context learning was already available without that feature: Brown et al. demonstrated it in 2020 (*NeurIPS*). Yiu, Kosoy and Gopnik’s study did not experimentally isolate persistent conversational memory as the cause of its results.

**Change:** Qualify this as a description of the tested systems, not an explanation of their performance. Add that the study did not test whether cross-conversation memory would reduce the gap. Keep evidence about newer models separate from evidence about memory: changing model generation and changing memory architecture are different interventions.

### 3. The new historical threshold conflicts with both your functional definition and your chosen system boundary

“It hasn't always been able to learn in use.”

“Before it, an AI assistant could learn for an hour and keep none of it, and ‘it doesn't really learn’ was close to true.”

There are two problems here.

First, learning within a conversation is still learning under a functional account. Its retention horizon is limited, but forgetting after a session does not retroactively make the preceding adaptation nearly nonexistent. You need to distinguish learning from retention across a particular administrative boundary: the start of a new conversation.

Second, once “it” includes tools, files and an agent’s surrounding software, consumer-app memory release dates cannot mark the beginning of the relevant capability. Reflexion used verbal feedback retained in an episodic memory to improve an agent’s subsequent attempts before 2024: Shinn et al., 2023, *NeurIPS*. Brown et al., 2020, *NeurIPS*, establishes the earlier in-context capability.

The strongest objection is internal: you cannot use the broad system boundary to include today’s file-writing agents, but effectively use the narrower consumer-app boundary when describing what earlier systems could do. The engine/car distinction requires consistency across time.

**Change:** Qualify the historical claim as the arrival of convenient, built-in persistence in particular consumer products. Cut the concession that “it doesn't really learn” was previously close to true. Distinguish within-context adaptation, retention across sessions and improvement across repeated tasks rather than treating memory’s product launch as a categorical transition into learning.

### 4. The final discovery question needs an explicit standard of novelty

“Ask whether it can find out what nobody has told it.”

This is defensible if it means inferring an unstated fact or mechanism about the particular environment being investigated. It becomes a substantially different claim if it means discovering something absent from the model’s training, or something no human has previously known.

The strongest objection is that neither developmental discovery nor scientific discovery requires being free of inherited concepts. A child can discover how this machine works while using familiar causal hypotheses. Likewise, a model can discover the rule of a new simulated machine even if conjunctions, blicket tasks and experimental strategies were extensively represented in training. Conversely, an answer not explicitly supplied in the prompt does not by itself establish effective causal exploration.

Lucas et al. (2014, *Cognition*) concerns learning and transferring expectations about causal forms. Gopnik et al. (2004, *Psychological Review*, “A theory of causal learning in children: causal maps and Bayes nets”) distinguishes the evidence and inferential operations involved in causal learning. Neither makes discovery equivalent to historical originality.

Your study can therefore investigate discovery without establishing unprecedented invention. But “nobody has told it” needs to specify what was withheld, from which component of the system, and over what period.

**Change:** Add an operational definition of discovery. Distinguish identifying an unstated mechanism, selecting informative interventions and transferring a learned expectation. Qualify the ending so that it neither demands human-unprecedented discoveries from AI nor treats every unstated answer as evidence of the developmental capacities at issue.

## MISREPRESENTATIONS

The new conclusion misrepresents Gopnik’s position when it says, “The case against rests on form.” The body already acknowledges that her published distinction is functional. The ending should retain that acknowledgment rather than converting it back into a biological or architectural objection.

“Tested with no memory of past conversations” may accurately describe a deployment limitation, but it risks misrepresenting the evidential meaning of Yiu, Kosoy and Gopnik’s results. It should not suggest that their study demonstrated a memory-related deficit, or that adding persistent memory answers the comparison they conducted.

The pre-memory concession also understates what earlier systems could do under your own whole-system definition. External-memory agents existed before the consumer features you date to 2024–25. This is not an objection that notes are “merely files”; it is an objection that your historical category changes halfway through the argument.

## WHAT THE PIECE GETS RIGHT THAT YOUR SIDE SHOULD CONCEDE

Frozen weights do not entail that the surrounding system cannot learn. Retaining and subsequently using a rule inferred through its own interventions can count as learning; external storage alone does not disqualify it.
