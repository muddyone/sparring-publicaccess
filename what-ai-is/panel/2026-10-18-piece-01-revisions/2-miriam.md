**Miriam Vale: argument review of piece 01's revisions, 2026-10-18**

The new "it" section is the right move. Naming the unit and dating the claim make the position easier to test and more honest. But the car framing doesn't yet fit the piece's second learner. The concession also gives away more than the brief supports. Ranked:

**1. The line of models has no place in the car.**
Current words: "When I say 'it,' I mean the whole car."
Problem: "Which thing learns" and "Where I land" both credit a second learner, the line of models. That is a line of engines, not a car. "It learns ... and the line of models learns across versions" then uses "it" for both. A careful reader will see the unit move.
Fix: add after "the whole car": "The engines also improve from one model to the next. That is a second learner, which I take up below, and I'll call it 'the line of models', not 'it'."

**2. The concession drops the brief's limit.**
Current words: "Before it, an AI assistant could learn for an hour and keep none of it, and 'it doesn't really learn' was close to true."
Problem: the brief says this was close to true "of the assistant in use, though never of in-conversation learning or of the line of models" (marked [inference]). Without that limit, the concession works against the piece's own second-grader reply to Shardlow. The concession itself helps, because it shows the position is dated and could be proved wrong. Keep it, with the limit.
Fix: "...and 'it doesn't really learn' was close to true of the assistant in use, though not of the line of models, which learned from version to version throughout."

**3. The opening sentence contradicts the next one.**
Current words: "It hasn't always been able to learn in use. A model could always pick up a pattern from examples within one conversation..."
Problem: by the piece's own definition, learning within one conversation is learning in use. What was missing was a lasting change.
Fix: "It hasn't always been able to keep what it learns." Also change "could always" to "has, since at least 2020, been able to". The brief dates in-context learning to GPT-3 in 2020, not "always".

**4. The Gopnik clause points the wrong way.**
Current words: "Those were 2022 and 2023 models, tested with no memory of past conversations, and newer ones have narrowed the gap."
Problem: the fact is right (brief, [inference]). But her tasks were single questions, and memory across conversations would not have come into them. Read as written, it sounds like an excuse for the models. Also, the newer tests that narrowed the gap were run without memory too. That narrowing came from the line of models, which actually helps your second learner.
Fix: "Those were 2022 and 2023 models, each answering single questions where memory of past conversations wouldn't come into it. Newer models, also tested without memory, have narrowed the gap (below)."

**5. "It does." drops the condition the piece set.**
Current words: "So stop asking whether it learns. It does."
Problem: the body argues "judged by function". The closing states it flat. Also, "the line of models learns across versions from how each one did" says more than the body, which says what shapes a new model "includes how its predecessor did".
Fix: "...the line of models learns across versions, partly from how each one did." And: "Judged by what it does, whether it learns is settled. The open question is whether it can find out what nobody has told it."

**6. "At least one model can" overstates the study.**
Current words: "My small study suggests that at least one model can find a rule in its own trials, write it down, and use it on the next task."
Problem: "can" is a claim about ability, and the evidence is one task, with the effect appearing in the third exploratory version, ten runs with notes and ten without, and notes sorted without a blind check. The body's own wording is narrower: "one model on one task."
Fix: "My small, exploratory study found one model, on one task, finding a rule in its own trials, writing it down, and using it on the next task. That needs a confirmation run before it carries weight."

**7. The date for written notes goes beyond the brief.**
Current words: "AI setups that work on their own began keeping written notes."
Problem: placed after "2025", it implies notes began then. The brief says when CLAUDE.md files first appeared "is not established". Research agents with memory came earlier.
Fix: "and by 2025, AI setups that work on their own were keeping written notes."

**8. "Everything in this piece" is too wide, and the landing leaves out the new scope.**
Current words: "Everything in this piece is about the system since then."
Problem: the piece also covers 2022–23 models, the line of models across all its versions, and a study whose notes ran in my own test setup, not a product's memory. Meanwhile "Where I land" says only "an AI assistant with a memory". It leaves out agent setups and the date.
Fix: change to "My claims are about the system since then." In "Where I land", change "an AI assistant with a memory" to "an AI system that keeps memory, whether an assistant or an agent setup that keeps notes".
