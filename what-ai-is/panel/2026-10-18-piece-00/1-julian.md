**Julian Crest: craft review of the series overview, "The thing on the other end"**

**Verdict.** The opening works. The diagnosis paragraph is the best writing in the piece: "both go wrong the same way" followed by "I think that is the wrong yardstick" gives the reader the series and its reason inside one screen. The middle sags, though, and the piece ends on a signpost, not a beat.

**Structure**

1. **"The method" repeats "The yardstick".** "The yardstick" already states the four moves. Then a new heading opens a one-item list that states them again (calculator standard, "compared to what?", the checks). A heading with one bullet under it reads as scaffolding. Fold the piece-01 bullet into the end of "The yardstick" as a prose link and drop the heading. That gives a cleaner run: yardstick, then yardstick applied, then what it finds, then why it matters to me.
2. **The "By 'it' I mean…" paragraph stalls the momentum** just after the four moves land. It is needed, but it belongs after the yardstick paragraph has handed off to piece 01, or in a short scoping line before the table.
3. **"What the yardstick keeps finding" earns its place, but should shrink.** The bold claim and the four bullets are the payoff, because they show the method producing a result. The closing paragraph ("The same extended-mind idea shows up in two forms…") repeats bullets one and two. Cut it, and keep its last sentence as a fifth bullet or as the section's closing line: "How much personality you give an AI assistant is partly a choice about which of those you want." That sentence is the only new thing in the paragraph.
4. **The ending.** The research paragraph is the right place to end. But its last sentence is a table-of-contents line, so the piece ends by telling the reader what to expect. Edit 10 below turns it back to the purpose stated in the opening.

**Line edits**

1. "These pieces are an honest account" -> "This series is an honest account"
   *It names the thing in a way that still makes sense when this page is read first, aloud, or on LinkedIn.*

2. "Each piece makes the same move." -> "Every piece applies the same yardstick."
   *One "move" followed by four moves is a miscount, and "yardstick" picks up the heading.*

3. "Ask \"compared to what?\", measured against whoever would otherwise fill that role, as they actually are." -> "Ask \"compared to what?\": measure it against whoever would otherwise fill that role, as they actually are."
   *The participle dangles. The other three moves are commands, so this one should be too.*

4. "## The method" -> (delete the heading; the bullet becomes a closing prose sentence of "The yardstick": "The first piece, [The wrong yardstick](../../positions/01-the-yardstick.md), builds that method: …")
   *This is structure note 1.*

5. Piece-02 bullet, whole text -> "Judged by what it does, an AI system with memory learns: it keeps what experience gives it and uses it later. The question worth asking, after Alison Gopnik, is whether it can discover what nobody told it. Between model releases it keeps what it learns outside the model, mostly in writing. In an exploratory study where the rules were never named, Claude wrote down a rule it had found (\"needs two\") and, with those notes, got the final task fully right 10 times out of 10, against 2 without them. GPT's notes did not carry the rule. One task, two models, found on the third version of the task, so a confirmation run designed in advance comes before any firm claim."
   *Brings it near the other bullets' length. Every number and every limit is kept, including "third version", which I must not soften. The one thing dropped is "every learner has a profile", which the piece itself carries.*

6. "Applied this way, the pieces kept arriving at the same unit." -> "Applied piece by piece, the yardstick kept pointing at the same thing."
   *"Unit" is jargon to a listener, and the subject should be the yardstick, which is the spine.*

7. "The same extended-mind idea shows up in two forms. … The more agent-like and social it becomes, the less it is the former." -> (cut)
   *It repeats bullets one and two (structure note 3).*

8. "How much personality you give an AI assistant is partly a choice about which of those you want." -> keep it, as the last line of the section: "Somewhere between part of your own mind and someone else you work with: how much personality you give it is partly a choice about which you want."
   *This puts back the "part of your mind / someone else" contrast that the cut takes out, so the line still stands on its own.*

9. "I have a stake in the answers, which is exactly why the yardstick matters:" -> "I have a stake in the answers, which is exactly why I need the yardstick:"
   *It makes the stake personal and active, and reads better aloud.*

10. "Each piece ends with what would change my mind." -> "So each piece ends with what would change my mind. An honest account has to say that too."
    *The ending goes back to the first sentence, so the piece ends on its purpose, not on a signpost.*

**Checked for faithfulness.** The piece-02 summary matches what piece 02 leads with: function over form, Gopnik, and discovery as the testable question. The piece-01 summary also matches its piece: the calculator standard, the curiosity gap, and "comparison, yes; deception, no". No edit above changes a claim or a number.

**What to do.** Make edits 4 and 7 first, because the argument tightens most there. Edit 10 is the cheapest way to make the piece end on a beat.
