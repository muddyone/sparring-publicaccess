**Verdict:** the overview's structure works for a newcomer. Three things still block a listener: the theory sections near the end lean on philosophy terms nobody has explained, the table won't read well aloud, and "it" carries too many meanings. Ten edits below, most important first. Impact is out of 10.

1. **Table, lines 41–46 (Impact 9).** Text-to-speech will read it as "Row. The usual question: Does it learn the way we do? The better question: …". Replace it with four plain lines: "Not 'Does it learn the way we do?' but 'What can it keep, and can it discover what nobody told it?'" Do the same for the other three rows. The meaning is unchanged and it reads aloud cleanly.

2. **"in the interpretive sense, a "quasi-subject"" → "in the sense that its behaviour is best explained as having beliefs and goals, a 'quasi-subject', almost a someone"** (Impact 8). Neither term means anything to a lay reader. A podcast voice will also drop the quote marks, so "quasi-subject" lands as an unexplained word.

3. **"on Clark and Chalmers' extended-mind argument" → "on the argument, from the philosophers Andy Clark and David Chalmers, that a tool you rely on the way you rely on memory can count as part of your mind"** (Impact 8). Spell the idea out here. Lines 89–90 can then keep "extended-mind idea" as a label for something already explained.

4. **"the interlocutor is not the model, which everyone shares, but the *thread*" → "the one you're actually talking to is not the model, which everyone shares, but the *thread*: your particular ongoing conversation"** (Impact 8). "Interlocutor" is jargon. "Thread" needs its meaning attached the first time it appears, and italics don't come through in audio.

5. **"The more agent-like and social it becomes, the less it is the former." → "The more the assistant acts on its own and the more social it becomes, the less it is part of your own mind."** (Impact 7). "It" has no clear antecedent, and "the former" asks a listener to recall the earlier list, which they can't.

6. **"because standard benchmarks didn't catch it" → "because the standard tests used to score AI models didn't catch it"** (Impact 6). This defines "benchmarks" and also fixes a second dangling "it".

7. **"The harm shapes are product properties that can be designed against" → "The ways the harm happens come from how the products are built, so they can be designed out"** (Impact 6). "Harm shapes" will confuse a lay reader and is easy for a voice to garble.

8. **"Alison Gopnik's real distinction is between passing knowledge along and discovering it, and every learner has a profile." → "The psychologist Alison Gopnik distinguishes passing knowledge along from discovering it. Every learner does some of each, in its own mix."** (Impact 5). This introduces Gopnik to someone hearing her name for the first time, and replaces the vague "profile". If "some of each" says more than the piece claims, keep "has its own profile" and add "of strengths". The other change is splitting one long sentence into two.

9. **"Ask "compared to what?", measured against whoever would otherwise fill that role, as they actually are." → "Ask 'compared to what?': compare it with whoever would otherwise do that job, as they really are, not at their best."** (Impact 5). The current sentence stacks clauses in a way that's hard to follow by ear. "Not at their best" only spells out what "as they actually are" already says, but please confirm it doesn't go further than the piece does.

10. **"the summary of piece 05" → "the summary of piece five"** (Impact 4). Text-to-speech says "zero five". This applies to any spoken number in the Notes. The same section also has a 40-word sentence starting "Every specific claim…". Split it after "citation by citation."
