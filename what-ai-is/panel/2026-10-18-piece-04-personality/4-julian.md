**Verdict.** The revision isn't patched, but it is heavy in two places: the start of the evidence section and the first Where I land paragraph. The opening works. **Opening.** It lands. The bold claim, "the difference between a long day and a short one", and "partly right, about something different" cover the ground in four sentences. The scope paragraph slows things down because it does three jobs: it defines personality, sets aside consistency and memory, and defines "it" as the series does. It's tolerable because each sentence is short. I'd keep it and fix only the stray "piece".

**Evidence section.** The sample sizes are fine, since they size the claims. What reads as heavy is the formatting. In the first list every bullet is a fully bolded sentence, while the second list uses labels ("Humanlike robots:"). Three bolded sentences in a row shout, and the two lists don't match. The recap paragraph also opens by repeating the heading two lines above it. One gap is left: the Fang caveat ("made no difference to loneliness or dependence") doesn't say how it bears on "the working day is an outcome". A reader may stop there. I can't bridge it without changing the claim, so I'm only flagging it.

**The example.** It works as the turn. "It was kind about it, and it didn't budge" is the strongest last sentence in the body, and it sets up "never at the cost of candor". One thing is unclear: "when it was the opposite" only makes sense to someone who has read essay 02. The author should decide whether "the opposite" can be named ("a case of *not* learning"?) without changing what happened. The section's heading says cost but it ends on the remedy. That's acceptable.

**Where I land.** Paragraph 1 packs three instructions (test candor, doubt in its own voice, modest by default) into one block, and the testing sentence reads like a protocol. Paragraph 2 then turns to friendship and Aristotle, so the essay's last full thought before the closing line is "Calling it a friend is where the claim stops holding." The closing line is about day versus work and testing, so it lands on a paragraph about something else. **Structural suggestion (not one of the line edits):** put the co-worker and Aristotle paragraph first, then the candor test, then the voice and modesty paragraph. That way "small acts like greetings" leads into "improve the first", and the test leads into "test that it never costs the second".
