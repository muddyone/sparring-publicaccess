_Substrate: Claude (subagent as Nadia Okonkwo), general-reader cold read. Piece 04._

I read it cold, as a curious general reader. I didn't edit anything.

**1. What it argues, and what "it" is.** If you give an AI assistant a personality, people like it more and keep using it, but it doesn't do the work any better. A nicer day still counts for something, as long as the warmth never softens what the assistant tells you is true. "It" means a chatbot-style AI helper you work alongside, with a tone and a name. Robots and exercise-coach apps come in only as evidence.

**2. Did I know what the words meant?** Mostly yes. "Personality" was never defined, but I got a working sense: tone, small talk, humour, a name, remembering you. Two things got muddy. In "Why it feels the way it does", consistency and memory each get credited with part of the benefit, and I lost track of what "personality" still covered once those were taken out. "Co-worker", "assistant" and "agent" also switch around. I got used to it, but "agent" made me pause the first time.

**3. Where I slowed down, got lost, or doubted it.**
- "Feeling related at work." I thought "related" meant family at first. "Connected" would have read better.
- "The cheapest model that works" (Dennett). I slowed down here. "Model" here means a mental picture, but in a piece about AI I first read it as an AI model.
- "Common ground... builds shorthand. In an assistant, that comes from memory, not personality." Fine, but I didn't know these assistants can remember me between conversations. Can they?
- The Kreiner aside, "who finds the effects differ by direction", lost me completely. Direction of what?
- "psychological safety" and "cohesion": I knew them only roughly. "sycophantic": I know the word, but it is the one person-facing term here that a lay reader might trip on.
- "(… the subscale figures come from a secondary summary)": this means nothing to me. It reads like a note for a scholar.
- "The failure my SPARRING work exists to catch." I have no idea what SPARRING is. It sounds like a product plug at just the moment I was starting to trust the piece.
- I believed the warmth-makes-it-less-accurate finding, and it was the most interesting part. I briefly doubted "Nature" and "2026" (is that real?) until "What would change my mind" admitted it hasn't been repeated yet. That admission earned my trust back.

**4. Does it end cleanly or drift?** It drifts. The strongest closing beat is point 6: "Calling it a friend is where the claim stops holding." That line stuck. But after it comes a paragraph about companion AI, then "What would change my mind", then notes. The companion paragraph in particular reads like a fence-post rather than an ending. The title line, "Both count", is the real thesis, and it never comes back.

**5. What I'd miss if handed only this piece.**
- Who "I" is, and what SPARRING is.
- What "the companion piece" (03) argues about whether an AI can wish you well. It is linked, but the friendship point leans on it.
- Whether today's assistants actually have memory and settings I can change. "Let the person set how much personality they want" assumes a dial I don't know I have.
- Some practical sense of what "warm in manner, blunt in verdicts" looks like in an actual reply. One short example exchange would have made the whole argument land.
- The notes say "Not yet reviewed by a panel". That made me wonder whether I was reading a draft.
