# Miriam Vale: revision plan for piece 04 ("Personality improves the relationship, not the work. Both count.")

**Diagnosis.** The piece's best idea holds up. A better working day is a real outcome, and warmth can quietly cost accuracy. But the piece concludes more than it shows. It counts four narrow results, two of which tested something other than personality, as a flat "no". It leaves out the human-side evidence that points the other way. And it ends three sections after its best line.

**1. "It's no" (Vera 1, Rhea O1). Impact 9.**
- **Current:** "So the question 'does personality improve output?' has an answer, and it's no."
- **Fix:** Use Vera's restatement: "So does personality improve the work? Nobody has shown that it does. Where it was measured (factual answers, robot tasks, exercise), it didn't help, and in one study warmth made answers worse. No one has yet tested it on real work over months."
- **Human side: yes, acknowledge it.** Van den Broeck's .26 for task performance comes from the same meta-analysis the piece already cites for commitment. Leaving it out is selective. Add to the first bullet: "…and, more weakly, with task performance (.26)." After the Methot bullet, add: "Friend groups outperform acquaintance groups, but on tasks that need a lot of output, not on tasks with one right answer (Chung et al. 2018, in the report)."
- **Title:** It now claims more than the body. "Personality helps the relationship. It hasn't been shown to help the work. Both count." Bart's call.

**2. "Personality" is never defined (Nadia; Vera 2, 8). Impact 8.**
- **Fix,** after the opening paragraph: "By personality I mean the social layer: a steady tone, a name, humor, small talk, greetings. Not what it knows or what it remembers. Consistency and memory help too, and I credit them separately below."
- **Fix,** in the nulls: use Vera's Zheng restatement ("Prompts that give a model a role ('You are a lawyer')…"), and add "These test something near personality, not personality itself." Use her Roesler restatement as well (robots viewed more favorably, no task gain, mostly single sessions).

**3. Ending (Nadia; series pattern). Impact 8.**
- **Order:** Move "What would change my mind" above "Where I land", as in 01–03. Move the companion paragraph up to sit with the scope line (item 7).
- **Where I land:** Rewrite as roughly 150 words of prose that keeps items 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6, and closes on "Calling it a friend is where the claim stops holding."
- **Last line:** "The day counts and the work counts. Let personality improve the first, and test that it never costs the second."

**4. Warmth and candor as a requirement, not a property (Rhea O2, O3). Impact 7.**
- **Current:** "It never touches candour, accuracy or the willingness to disagree. Those stay fixed at every setting."
- **Fix:** "It must never touch candor, accuracy or the willingness to disagree. That is a requirement to test, not something you can assume. The warmth study shows they drift." In the test item, add "weakened warnings and dropped corrections".
- **Current:** "whether you come back, whether you trust and use what you have"
- **Fix:** "and whether you keep using a tool that is actually good for the work". The piece later shows that coming back and trusting are the signals sycophancy exploits. It cannot count them as gains here.

- "Competence first" is already in the report (McAllister). If Bart wants the point kept, use Vera's hedge in the body and drop it as a design rule.

Whether it can offer goodwill is unresolved ([03](../../positions/03-someone-there.md)). So 'a good co-worker, made pleasant' is the honest claim." Send this to `/cite-check`.
