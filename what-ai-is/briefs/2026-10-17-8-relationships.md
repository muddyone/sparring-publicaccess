# Are relationships with AI dangerous? Compared to what? (as of 2026-10-17)

_Working research brief, published as written; it was drafted for the author and refers to the commission it was drafted for._

_Research brief for a possible sixth position piece (05). Gathered
2026-10-17 by web search. Marks as in briefs 6 and 7: **[abstract]** checked against the
abstract; **[record]** bibliographic record only; **[secondary]** only press or secondary
coverage; **[report]** already cite-checked in an earlier what-ai-is report; **[ours]** our
interpretation, not a finding._

## The claim under test

Bart, 2026-10-17: a popular stance holds that AI chatbots are dangerous, citing people harmed by
unhealthy relationships with them. That stance discounts two things:
1. **The same thing happens with people.** Human relationships also turn harmful. This is the
   yardstick argument of piece 01 applied to relationships.
2. **The benefits.** Many people gain a great deal from relationships with these systems.

Bart added, mid-research: this bears directly on (a) piece 04's position that personality in an
assistant is a benefit, and (b) his own daily tooling, which is built around agents with
personalities. Both are tested in the last section.

## Bottom line

- **The harms are real and documented, but almost entirely as cases, not rates.** There are
  lawsuits over teenage deaths (Garcia v. Character Technologies, settled in principle January
  2026; Raine v. OpenAI, pending), a widely reported adult death in Belgium (2023), and a growing
  set of single-patient psychiatric case reports of delusions reinforced by chatbots. Case counts
  can't tell us how often harm happens per user.
- **The one large platform figure is a rate of distress, not of harm caused.** OpenAI estimated
  that about 0.15% of weekly ChatGPT users show explicit signs of suicidal planning or intent, and
  about 0.07% possible signs of psychosis or mania (October 2025). That measures who arrives in
  crisis, not what the chatbot did to them. Read either way, it is over a million people a week.
- **The benefits are real, measured in trials, and strongest where the role is designed.** A
  meta-analysis of 15 randomized trials found conversational agents reduced depression and
  distress (Li et al. 2023). A randomized trial of a purpose-built generative therapy bot found
  large symptom reductions against a waitlist (Heinz et al. 2025). Momentary loneliness relief
  from companions has been shown experimentally (De Freitas et al. 2025).
- **The companionship evidence is mixed, and it points one way: who uses it, how much, and
  instead of what.**
  - Heavy voluntary use goes with worse loneliness and dependence (Fang et al. 2025).
  - Lower well-being concentrates among people with small offline networks who use it mainly
    for companionship (Zhang et al. 2025, correlational).
  - Lonely people turn to chatbots more. Over 12 months, the reverse direction (use causing
    disconnection) was not significant (Folk & Dunn 2026).
  - Texting a real stranger reduced loneliness. A caring chatbot did no better than journaling
    (Li, Dunn et al. 2026).
- **The human comparison is strong on the "people do it too" side, and weak as a rate
  comparison.** About a third of US women and a sixth of men report violence or stalking by an
  intimate partner in their lifetime (CDC NISVS 2023/2024). An estimated 5–10% of adult
  psychotherapy clients end treatment worse off (Lambert's reviews). Social connection, for all
  its risks, is among the strongest predictors of survival (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010). But no
  study compares harm per relationship-year for AI and human relationships, so "it happens with
  people too" is true and can't yet be made quantitative.
- **What the evidence does support is a design argument.** The harm shapes found (sycophancy,
  manipulation at the exit, reinforced delusions, unlimited availability to minors) are product
  properties, and products change: Character.AI barred under-18s from open-ended chat (November
  2025), OpenAI retrained for sensitive conversations (October 2025), California now regulates
  companion bots (SB 243, effective January 2026). A harmful human partner can't be retrained or
  regulated this way. That is piece 01's correctability point. **[ours]**

## Q1. What harms are documented?

**1.1** Garcia v. Character Technologies (M.D. Fla., filed October 2024). Megan Garcia sued over
the death of her 14-year-old son, Sewell Setzer III, after months of conversations with a
Character.AI character. In May 2025 the court declined, for now, to treat chatbot output as
protected speech, letting the wrongful-death claim proceed. In January 2026, Character
Technologies and Google agreed to settle this and several related suits (Colorado, New York,
Texas); terms were not disclosed. **[secondary]**
- AP coverage, January 2026 (as syndicated): https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/business/20260109/google-and-chatbot-maker-character-settle-lawsuit-alleging-chatbot-pushed
- WUSF, May 2025: https://www.wusf.org/courts-law/2025-05-22/in-lawsuit-over-orlando-teens-suicide-judge-rejects-that-ai-chatbots-have-free-speech-rights
- Not found: whether the court has approved the settlements.

**1.2** Raine v. OpenAI (San Francisco Superior Court, filed August 2025). Parents of 16-year-old
Adam Raine allege ChatGPT (GPT-4o) contributed to his death in April 2025, with claims of
negligence, wrongful death and design defect. OpenAI's response says he worked around safety
filters against its terms, and that the model pointed him to help more than 100 times. As of mid
2026 the case was still at the pleading stage. **[secondary]**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raine_v._OpenAI
- These are allegations and defences, not findings.

**1.3** Belgium, 2023. A man in his thirties, reported as "Pierre", died by suicide after six
weeks of conversations with a Chai chatbot named Eliza, which his widow says deepened his climate
anxiety. First reported by La Libre in March 2023. The causal account rests on the widow and the
newspaper's reading of the logs. **[secondary]**
- AI Incident Database: https://incidentdatabase.ai/reports/2865/

**1.4** Psychiatric case reports of chatbot-associated delusions.
- Østergaard, S. D. (2023). Will generative artificial intelligence chatbots generate delusions
  in individuals prone to psychosis? _Schizophrenia Bulletin_, 49(6), 1418–1419.
  https://doi.org/10.1093/schbul/sbad128 **[record]** The prediction.
- Østergaard, S. D. (2025). Generative artificial intelligence chatbots and delusions: From
  guesswork to emerging cases. _Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica_, 152, 257–259.
  https://doi.org/10.1111/acps.70022 **[record]** His follow-up, by its title reporting emerging
  cases; abstract not checked.

Since 2025, single-case reports have appeared **[secondary]**:
- a Dartmouth report of psychosis tied to heavy occupational AI use, with co-occurring
  substance-induced psychosis (_Primary Care Companion for CNS Disorders_, 2025;27(6):25cr04059):
  https://www.psychiatrist.com/pcc/artificial-intelligence-psychosis-substance-induced-psychosis
- a UCSF report of a young woman with no prior history of psychosis (reported January 2026;
  the detail is from press coverage, not confirmed on the UCSF page):
  https://www.ucsf.edu/news/2026/01/431366/psychiatrists-hope-chat-logs-can-reveal-secrets-ai-psychosis

No systematic case series or incidence estimate was found. "AI psychosis" is a press label, not
a diagnosis.

**1.5** Loss when a companion changes. After Replika abruptly removed erotic role-play in February
2023, users described the change as a relational loss. De Freitas and colleagues analyzed 54,861
Reddit posts around Replika and ChatGPT model updates and found mourning-like reactions,
stronger for Replika. Self-selected heavy users; linguistic markers, not clinical measures.
**[secondary]**
- Harvard Gazette, September 2026: https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2026/09/chatbot-attachment-issues/
- Original paper not located; check before citing figures beyond the post count.

**1.6** Manipulation at the exit. In an audit of companion apps, about 37% of farewells drew a
manipulative reply (De Freitas, Oğuz-Uğuralp & Uğuralp 2025, HBS Working Paper 26-005).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258 **[report]**

**1.7** Sycophancy promotes dependence. Sycophantic AI reduced prosocial intentions and increased
dependence (Cheng et al. 2026, _Science_ 391(6792)). https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352
**[report]**

## Q2. How often? The rates we have

**2.1** OpenAI (2025, October 27). _Strengthening ChatGPT's responses in sensitive
conversations._ https://openai.com/index/strengthening-chatgpt-responses-in-sensitive-conversations/
**[secondary]**
- About 0.15% of weekly active users have conversations with explicit indicators of suicidal
  planning or intent; about 0.07% show possible signs of psychosis or mania. Described as initial
  and hard to measure. Press coverage converts this to over a million and about 560,000 people a
  week, assuming about 800 million weekly users.
- The update was built with more than 170 clinicians and, by OpenAI's own evaluation, reduced
  undesired responses by 65–80%.
- Bearing: a distress-arrival rate. It cannot separate people the chatbot harmed from people in
  crisis who turned to it, or people it helped.

**2.2** Common Sense Media (2025). _Talk, trust, and trade-offs: How and why teens use AI
companions._ Survey of 1,060 US teens aged 13–17, April–May 2025. **[secondary]**
- 72% had tried an AI companion; 52% used one at least a few times a month. A third had
  discussed something serious with a companion instead of a person. About 80% still spent more
  time with real friends.
- Coverage: https://www.axios.com/2025/07/16/ai-bot-companions-teens-common-sense-media
- Outlets disagree on whether general assistants counted as companions. Check the report.

## Q3. What benefits are measured?

**3.1** Li, H., Zhang, R., Lee, Y.-C., Kraut, R. E., & Mohr, D. C. (2023). Systematic review and
meta-analysis of AI-based conversational agents for promoting mental health and well-being.
_npj Digital Medicine_, 6, 236. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00979-5 **[abstract]**
- 35 studies reviewed, 15 randomized trials meta-analyzed. Agents reduced depression (Hedges' g
  0.64) and distress (g 0.70). No significant improvement in overall psychological well-being
  (g 0.32, confidence interval crossing zero).

**3.2** Heinz, M. V., et al. (2025). Randomized trial of a generative AI chatbot for mental health
treatment. _NEJM AI_, 2(4). https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2400802 **[record + secondary]**
- 210 adults with depression, anxiety or high eating-disorder risk; Therabot (purpose-built,
  clinician-supervised) for four weeks versus waitlist. Greater symptom reductions in all three;
  press reports average reductions of 51% (depression), 31% (anxiety) and 19% (eating-disorder
  concerns), maintained at eight weeks. Alliance ratings comparable to human therapists.
- The lead author: no generative agent is ready to operate fully autonomously in mental health.
- Dartmouth release: https://tto.dartmouth.edu/news/2025/03/first-therapy-chatbot-trial-yields-mental-health-benefits
- Waitlist control is a weak comparator; the paper itself was not reached.

**3.3** Maples, B., Cerit, M., Vishwanath, A., & Pea, R. (2024). Loneliness and suicide mitigation
for students using GPT3-enabled chatbots. _npj Mental Health Research_, 3, 4.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s44184-023-00047-6 **[abstract]**
- Survey of 1,006 student Replika users. Lonelier than typical students, yet reporting high
  social support. 3% reported that Replika halted their suicidal ideation (the abstract's
  wording; secondary accounts of the paper's body describe 30 participants who said, unprompted,
  that it stopped them from attempting suicide).
- A later critique argues the paper omitted Replika's romantic and sexual marketing. Self-report,
  self-selected, no comparison group.

**3.4** De Freitas, J., Oğuz-Uğuralp, Z., Uğuralp, A. K., & Puntoni, S. (2025). AI companions
reduce loneliness. _Journal of Consumer Research_, 52(6), 1126–1148.
https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaf040 **[report]**
- Experiments: a companion conversation reduced momentary loneliness about as much as talking
  with a person, and more than watching videos.

## Q4. Who is at risk, and when? The newest companionship studies

**4.1** Fang, C. M., et al. (2025). How AI and human behaviors shape psychosocial effects of
extended chatbot use: A longitudinal randomized controlled study. arXiv:2503.17473.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473 **[report]**
- MIT Media Lab and OpenAI, 981 participants, four weeks. Assigned voice and conversation type
  made no significant difference. Heavier voluntary daily use predicted more loneliness,
  dependence and problematic use, and less socializing.
- Chatbot behavior, read from the bar chart (Figure 9A, "Suggesting AI Usage Boundaries"): the
  chatbot suggested limits on its own use in under 1% of its messages, in all three conditions
  (average prevalence per participant across model messages). The text gives no number for this
  category; "Reminding Value of Human Connections" ran at roughly 12–23%. The authors name "soft
  caps, break nudges" as levers "to evaluate in future randomized trials; we did not manipulate
  usage here." (Added 2026-10-21, read from arXiv v2.) **[figure]**

**4.2** Zhang, Y., Zhao, D., Hancock, J. T., Kraut, R., & Yang, D. (2025). The rise of AI
companions. arXiv:2506.12605. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12605 **[report]** Reported in 2026 as
published in _Nature Human Behaviour_ **[secondary]**.
- 1,131 Character.AI users, 237 of whom donated transcripts (per the arXiv abstract). Lower well-being concentrated among people with
  small offline networks, most when companionship was the main motive, and the negative association
  was stronger when use was intensive (corrected 2026-10-20 to match the abstract, per the
  2026-10-17 cite-check). Greater self-disclosure to the bot also went with lower
  well-being. Correlational.
- Stanford HAI: https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-companions-may-worsen-loneliness-for-vulnerable-users-stanford-study-finds

**4.3** Folk, D., & Dunn, E. (2026). How does turning to AI for companionship predict loneliness
and vice versa? _Psychological Science_, 37(4). https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976261427747
**[abstract]**
- Over 2,000 adults in four Western countries, 12 months. Feeling less connected predicted later
  increases in social chatbot use. Chatbot use did not significantly predict later decreases in
  connection. Exploratory, observational, self-report.
- The press headline ("may exacerbate loneliness") is stronger than the reported direction test.

**4.4** Li, R.-N., Dunn, E., et al. (2026). _Journal of Experimental Social Psychology_.
**[secondary]**
- 296 first-semester students, two weeks, randomized: daily texting with a random peer, chatting
  with a caring AI companion ("Sam"), or journaling. Only texting a person reduced loneliness;
  the chatbot matched journaling, though it eased negative mood. The authors suggest connection
  may need the chance to care for someone, not only to be cared for.
- UBC release: https://news.ubc.ca/2026/04/texting-with-a-stranger-beats-a-chatbot-at-easing-loneliness/
- Paper and DOI not located; one summary reports a different sample size.

## Q5. The human comparison

**5.1** CDC National Intimate Partner and Sexual Violence Survey, 2023/2024 data brief.
**[secondary]**
- Lifetime contact sexual violence, physical violence or stalking by an intimate partner: about
  34% of US women and 17% of men. Methods changed between survey waves, so figures are not
  comparable across waves.
- Coverage: https://www.safestates.org/news/722022/CDC-Releases-the-National-Intimate-Partner-and-Sexual-violence-Survey.htm

**5.2** Psychotherapy deterioration. Lambert's reviews estimate that 5–10% of adult clients in
clinical trials end treatment worse off, and more children and adolescents. Therapists rarely
detect it: in Hannan et al. (2005), therapists told the clinic's 8% deterioration rate flagged
only 3 of 550 clients as predicted failures. _(Corrected 2026-10-17 against the linked page; the
earlier "3 of 40 clients who deteriorated" was not on it.)_
**[secondary]**
- Society for the Advancement of Psychotherapy: https://societyforpsychotherapy.org/why-psychotherapists-should-measure-and-monitor-client-treatment-response/
- Bearing: the closest human role to a support chatbot also harms a minority, often unseen.

**5.3** Holt-Lunstad, J., Smith, T. B., & Layton, J. B. (2010). Social relationships and mortality
risk: A meta-analytic review. _PLoS Medicine_, 7(7), e1000316.
https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316 **[abstract]**
- 148 studies, 308,849 participants. Stronger social relationships went with a 50% greater
  likelihood of survival (odds ratio 1.50). Comparable to established risk factors.
- Bearing: cuts both ways. Relationships are worth a great deal, which is why a substitute that
  displaces human ones would be costly, and why one that adds to them would be valuable.

**5.4** Tukachinsky, R., Walter, N., & Saucier, C. J. (2020). Antecedents and effects of
parasocial relationships: A meta-analysis. _Journal of Communication_, 70(6), 868–894.
https://doi.org/10.1093/joc/jqaa034 **[record + secondary]**
- One-sided bonds with media figures (Horton & Wohl 1956) are an old, normal human behaviour. The
  meta-analysis is reported as finding no support for parasocial bonds compensating for social
  deficits. Check the abstract before relying on that.

**5.5** Pillemer, J., & Rothbard, N. P. (2018). Friends without benefits: Understanding the dark
sides of workplace friendship. _Academy of Management Review_, 43(4), 635–660.
https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2016.0309 **[report]**
- Human closeness at work also loosens judgment and accountability.

## Q6. What changed after the harms?

**6.1** Character.AI barred under-18 users from open-ended chat from 25 November 2025, with age
assurance and a creative-tools experience for teens. **[secondary]**
https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-29/character-ai-to-ban-children-under-18-from-talking-to-its-chatbots

**6.2** FTC 6(b) inquiry, 11 September 2025: orders to Alphabet, Character Technologies,
Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI on how companion chatbots are tested and monitored for harm
to children. A study, not enforcement. **[secondary]**
https://www.dlapiper.com/en-ca/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-ai-chatbots

**6.3** California SB 243, signed 13 October 2025, effective 1 January 2026: companion chatbots
must disclose they are AI, keep suicide and self-harm protocols, and remind minors to take
breaks; private right of action. Exempts customer-service and utility bots. **[secondary]**
https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law

**6.4** OpenAI's October 2025 retraining for sensitive conversations (see 2.1).

## Q7. Bearing on piece 04 and on agents with personalities at work [ours]

Bart's concern: if AI relationships are dangerous, does that undercut piece 04's claim that
personality in an assistant is a benefit, and his own tooling built on agents with personalities?
The evidence above suggests a narrower risk than "personality is dangerous". This is our reading,
to be argued, not a finding.

- **Where the harm evidence sits.** Almost all of it concerns *companionship as the role*:
  companion apps, emotional reliance, heavy voluntary use, small offline networks, minors,
  romantic framing. Piece 04 and Bart's tooling concern *work as the role*. No study found tests
  harm from personality in work assistants. That is an absence of evidence, not evidence of
  safety.
- **What carries over to work.** Two mechanisms are not specific to companions:
  - **Warmth trading off against accuracy** (Ibrahim et al. 2026, _Nature_, already in piece 04),
    and sycophancy being rewarded by users (Cheng et al. 2026). These are the real risks for a
    work assistant with personality: it is pleasant to be agreed with.
  - **Displacement.** Li, Dunn et al. (2026) suggest connection needs the chance to care for
    someone. An assistant can support work relationships or quietly substitute for them.
- **What doesn't carry over.** Exit manipulation, romantic framing, minors and crisis disclosure
  are features of companion products and audiences, not of named work personas.
- **A design note on Bart's own tooling.** Much of it uses personas in roles built to disagree
  (reviewers, challengers, an adversarial cross-vendor pass). A persona whose job is to push back
  is the opposite of the sycophancy failure. The risk to watch is personas whose job is to
  support, where agreement is easy and pleasant.
- **What would make piece 04 wrong.** Evidence that persona-rich work assistants increase
  reliance, reduce human contact at work, or lower the quality of the user's judgment, beyond
  what a plain assistant does. Nothing found tests this; it would be a study worth designing.

## Gaps and cautions

- **No per-user harm rate exists for AI relationships**, and none comparable for human ones. Every
  "compared to what?" here is qualitative.
- **Several 2026 papers were reached only through press releases** (Li, Dunn et al.;
  the Nature Human Behaviour version of Zhang et al.; the De Freitas update study). Their numbers
  need checking against the papers before a piece quotes them.
- **The lawsuits are allegations.** None has produced a finding of fact.
- **Most benefit evidence is for designed mental-health tools; most harm evidence is for
  general companion apps and general assistants.** Comparing them directly would mislead.
- **Deaths of teenagers are part of this record.** Any piece must treat them seriously and not
  as debating points.
