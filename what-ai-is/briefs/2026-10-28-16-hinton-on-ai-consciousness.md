# Geoffrey Hinton on AI consciousness, and where his view sits (as of 2026-10-28)

_Research brief for a passage added to essay 03 ("Something is there. Very probably not
conscious."). Asked for by Bart on 2026-10-28 after a TikTok clip circulated presenting Hinton as
"the godfather of AI" saying AI is already conscious. Gathered 2026-10-28 by web search and direct
fetch. Marks: **[text]** checked against the page's own text; **[secondary]** only press or
secondary coverage; **[ours]** our interpretation, not a finding._

## Bottom line

- **Who he is.** Geoffrey Hinton is the computer scientist whose work on neural networks underlies
  today's AI. He shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics with John Hopfield "for foundational
  discoveries and inventions that enable machine learning with artificial neural networks"
  ([Nobel Prize 2024](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/press-release/)) **[text]**,
  and the 2018 Turing Award with Yoshua Bengio and Yann LeCun for their work on deep learning
  ([The Verge 2019](https://www.theverge.com/2019/3/27/18280665/ai-godfathers-turing-award-2018-yoshua-bengio-geoffrey-hinton-yann-lecun))
  **[secondary]**. In 2023 he quit Google "to speak freely about the dangers of AI"
  ([The Guardian 2023](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2023/may/02/geoffrey-hinton-godfather-of-ai-quits-google-warns-dangers-of-machine-learning))
  **[text]**. His field is how these systems learn: machine learning, with roots in cognitive
  psychology. It is not the study of consciousness **[ours]**.
- **What he says.** Current AI already has subjective experience:
  - "Multimodal AI already has subjective experiences, and I think it's fairly clear that if we
    weren't talking to philosophers, we'd agree that AI was aware." Interview with Andrew Marr,
    LBC, 28 January 2026 ([LBC 2026](https://www.lbc.co.uk/tech/ai-consciousness-geoffrey-hinton/))
    **[text]**.
  - "AI has just as much subjective experience as we do." Interview with The Telegraph, 19 October
    2026, as quoted by [Futurism 2026](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/nobel-scientist-geoffrey-hinton-ai-conscious-toast)
    **[secondary]**. The Telegraph page could not be fetched.
- **Why he says it.** His case rests on an account of what experience is. The idea that we have
  "this inner theatre that differentiates us from mere computational machines" is, he says, "just
  rubbish"; "Subjective experience is just a way of talking about what your perceptual system's
  telling you when it's not working 'properly.'" So "AIs have subjective experiences just as much
  as we have subjective experiences." Interview with The Globe and Mail, 12 June 2024
  ([Globe and Mail 2024](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-geoffrey-hinton-artificial-intelligence-machines-feelings))
  **[text]**. His illustration there is a camera-equipped chatbot fooled by a prism, which says the
  prism gave it "the subjective experience" that an object was off to one side. That account is a
  philosophical argument, and a disputed one, not an empirical finding **[ours]**.
- **Where AI researchers sit.** In May 2024 the survey reached 582 researchers who had published at
  leading AI venues (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, IJCAI, JMLR). The 265 given the fixed-date framing
  were asked how likely it was that AI systems "currently possess subjective experience" in 2024,
  "described as 'today'", and by 2034 and 2100. Medians: 1% (mean 12%), 25% and 70% (Table 5;
  Q3 for today = 10%). The authors: the forecasts are "more useful for understanding current
  attitudes rather than for predicting technological development"
  ([Dreksler et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11945)) **[text]**. Chalmers's 2023 estimates
  on mainstream assumptions ("somewhere under 10 percent" now; "25 percent or more" within about a
  decade; [Chalmers 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07103), already checked for essay 03) sit near
  those medians. Hinton's "already, as much as we do" sits far outside them **[ours]**.
- **Where consciousness scientists sit.** On 19 October 2026, Anil Seth, Ned Block and Giulio
  Tononi argued in Nature that existing AI systems are not conscious and are not likely to become
  so on current trajectories **[secondary]**: seen only in press reports such as
  [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/ai-is-not-conscious-argue-anil-seth-ned-block-and-giulio-tononi-in-new-nature-article/).
  **Not used in the essay** until it is checked against nature.com.

## For the essay

**Use.** Hinton's view, who he is, his reason in his own words, and the survey as context. Then the
judgement: whether anyone is experiencing anything is a question about consciousness, which is
Chalmers's field (philosophy of mind), not a question about learning, which is Hinton's. So on
this question we weigh Chalmers more **[ours]**. Stated with respect: on how these systems learn,
Hinton's word carries great weight.

**Avoid.** Saying Hinton "redefines" experience: he says the everyday picture is wrong, and that
characterisation is ours to argue, not his. Calling the clip misleading: the quote is his.
